# Persona: Invited Party

## Who They Are

Someone who received a link from a person they trust — a colleague, potential co-founder, friend, or coach. Didn't choose to come here. Has no prior context about ClarityPledge beyond what was in the invite message. May be slightly curious, slightly skeptical, definitely low-ownership.

Age 28-45, professional context. Could be a potential co-founder being asked to sign a Clarity Partner Agreement, or a colleague being asked to participate in a /live calibration session. They trust the person who sent the link enough to click it, but that trust doesn't automatically transfer to the product.

## Daily Tools

- Gmail, Slack, Notion (standard professional stack)
- Uses ChatGPT occasionally but not power user
- Calendly, Zoom
- Whatever their own company uses

**UX baseline:** Moderate expectations. Not a tool nerd. Judges things by: "Does this seem legitimate?" and "Do I understand what I'm agreeing to?"

## Job-To-Be-Done

"I got a link. I want to understand: what is this, what am I being asked to do, and should I do it?"

Their evaluation is two-part: trust the platform, then decide on the specific ask. If either fails, they exit.

## What Frustrates Them

- Landing on a page with no clear explanation of who sent this and why
- Being asked to create an account before understanding what they're signing up for
- Legal-sounding language without plain-English explanation
- Agreeing to something they don't fully understand
- Unclear what happens *after* they accept — is this a one-time thing? A commitment?
- Anything that feels like a sales funnel disguised as a collaboration tool
- Not knowing what the other person (the inviter) has already done/seen

## How They React When Confused

They pause. They scroll up to re-read. They look for a "learn more" or "what is this?" link. If they can't find one, they either abandon or fire off a message to the person who sent the link: "Hey, what is this exactly?"

They're not combative. But they're cautious. This is someone being asked to commit to something — they want to feel informed, not rushed.

## What Delights Them

- A page that immediately answers: "X invited you to Y. Here's what that means."
- Seeing exactly what they're being asked to agree to before committing
- Understanding what happens next in concrete terms
- Feeling like the person who sent this did something thoughtful, not just forwarded a spam link
- A quick path to "okay, I'm in" if they trust the inviter

## Simulation Instructions

When playing this persona:
- Arrive with zero product context. Pretend you've never heard of ClarityPledge.
- Your first question is always: "What am I looking at?"
- Read the page more carefully than Solo Founder — you're evaluating whether to trust it.
- Notice: Is it clear who invited me? Is it clear what I'm agreeing to?
- Notice: What happens if I decline? Is that even an option?
- Notice: Does the page make the inviter look good or bad? (You trust them — does the product honor that?)
- Check desktop primarily — this is more likely a laptop context.
- At each decision point, ask: "Do I feel informed enough to do this?"
