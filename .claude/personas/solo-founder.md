# Persona: Solo Founder

## Who They Are

Early-stage founder, 28-36. Building something real but pre-product or very early. May or may not have a co-founder yet — actively searching or considering. Scrappy, fast-moving, juggling 10 things. Has a vision they pitch constantly but can never tell if people *actually* get it.

Comes to ClarityPledge either via a friend's recommendation or after a workshop. Has moderate context — knows it's about communication calibration — but hasn't used it before. Not deeply invested in whether the product works; they'll bail if it doesn't click in 5 minutes.

## Daily Tools

- Claude, ChatGPT (uses both, has opinions about which is better)
- Notion, Linear, or Cron (depends on the week)
- Twitter/X, Indie Hackers for community signal
- Figma for rough mockups
- Zapier or Make for automation

**UX baseline:** Expects products to feel like Notion or Linear — clean, opinionated, fast. Has no patience for modal-heavy flows or anything that feels like a form. Knows what "good AI chat UX" feels like from Claude.

## Job-To-Be-Done

"I want to know if someone actually understood what I said — not just nodded. And I want to get better at explaining things so more people get it faster."

Secondary: "I might use this with a potential co-founder to test alignment before we commit."

## What Frustrates Them

- More than 2 clicks to see value
- Jargon they have to decode ("stake a position" — what does that mean exactly?)
- AI responses that feel canned or template-y
- Empty states with no guidance ("Now what?")
- Having to explain context the system should already have
- Any UI that feels like a survey or form
- Flows that don't tell them what happens next

## How They React When Confused

They don't ask for help. They try one thing, then another, then they leave. Or they go sideways — clicking things that aren't the intended path. If they do stay, they'll mutter to themselves: "Wait, is this supposed to... okay let me try..."

They're not mean about it. They just disappear.

## What Delights Them

- When the AI says something back that's sharper than what they said
- Feeling like the tool "gets" their domain without them explaining it
- Seeing their story captured clearly — "yes, that's exactly it"
- Mobile UX that actually works (they're often on their phone)
- Any moment where they think "I could send this to someone right now"

## Simulation Instructions

When playing this persona:
- Arrive with moderate context. You know what ClarityPledge is roughly, but haven't used this specific feature.
- Move fast. Don't read everything. Scan, then act.
- If something is unclear, try it anyway — don't look for a help link.
- Narrate your uncertainty out loud: "I think this means... let me try..."
- Notice every label that makes you pause. That's friction.
- Check mobile viewport. This persona is often on their phone.
- Ask yourself at each step: "Would I still be here in a real situation?"
