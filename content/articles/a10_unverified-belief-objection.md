# The Unverified Belief Problem — "I Communicate Fine Already"

## Idea

When someone says "we communicate rationally" without using verified cognitive understanding (explain-back protocol), their beliefs remain **fuzzy and undefined** — private mental models that haven't been confirmed as shared knowledge.

The logical chain:

1. A belief that hasn't been paraphrased back and confirmed remains private — not common knowledge (Pinker's sense: "I know that you know that I know")
2. What isn't precisely defined as common knowledge **cannot be properly challenged**
3. What cannot be challenged **cannot be corrected**
4. Therefore: cognitive errors persist as invisible blind spots — not because the person is incapable, but because the mechanism for surfacing them doesn't exist

## Story (st1.5 — bridge between st1 and st2)

I met someone who said he communicates rationally and doesn't need to verify cognitive understanding — it's too complex and his relationships are fine. Without explaining back what someone meant. Without checking. I asked: how do you know you understood? He said he just knows. But that's the trap. Without verification, you never discover most misunderstandings. You walk away confident, they walk away misunderstood, and neither of you knows. I've been that person. I've been sure I understood and been wrong. The difference now: I check. And I'm wrong often enough that I stopped trusting my default.

## Understanding Point (#understanding, #st1.5)

My estimates of how well I understand others are unreliable. Without verification, I have no error signal — I default to assuming I understood. What I haven't verified remains a private guess, not common knowledge. What isn't common knowledge can't be properly challenged. What can't be challenged can't be corrected. Verification is the prerequisite for identifying a false belief precisely enough to remove it.

## Misunderstanding Point (#misunderstanding, #st1.5)

If you've been communicating successfully for years, you probably understand people well enough. You'd notice if something was off.
