## Idea

Locke established consent as the basis of legitimate authority (1689). Every modern democracy is built on this. But consent without verified comprehension is meaningless — if a population votes for a policy they can't accurately paraphrase, is that legitimate governance? The same gap Popper left unfilled (falsification assumes transparent communication) exists in Locke's framework. ClarityPledge operationalizes the comprehension layer underneath consent: "Locke to Ladischenski" — from consent of the governed to comprehension of the governed. The article traces the progression: divine right → consent (Locke) → verified comprehension, and argues this isn't a small amendment but a structural upgrade to the social contract.
