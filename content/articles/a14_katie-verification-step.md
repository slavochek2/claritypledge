Arc: ARC-4 (The Assumption Nobody Questioned)

## Idea

Byron Katie's "The Work" asks four questions about a painful thought, starting with "Is it true?" David Burns' CBT identifies cognitive distortions and corrects them against evidence. Both are powerful — but both are closed loops. You're alone with your thought, auditing yourself with your own assumptions.

ClarityPledge opens the loop. Instead of "is it true?" the question becomes: "does someone who *verifiably understands what I mean* see it differently?" This is a fundamentally stronger epistemic move because it introduces an actual falsification condition.

**Three epistemological audits at different levels:**
- Burns verifies *accuracy* of thought (is this distortion or reality?)
- Katie verifies *ownership* of thought (whose business is this?)
- ClarityPledge verifies *comprehension* of thought (did the other person understand my intent before responding?)

**The key insight:** None of them ever verify that comprehension actually occurred. Burns assumes you understood the reframe. Katie assumes you understood the question. Ellis assumes you understood the "must" vs "prefer" distinction. They all operate on assumed comprehension — exactly the failure mode ClarityPledge names.

**Katie-with-a-comprehension-gate:** Someone who demonstrably understands what you mean offers the alternative view. You reproduce *their* view back to them. In that reproduction, your position moves — not because they persuaded you, but because you couldn't hold both comprehensions simultaneously without updating. That's not therapy. That's an epistemological intervention with a verification step.

## Structure (draft)

1. The thought — pick a universal example (not personal)
2. Walk it through Burns: here's what he'd do (identify distortions, correct)
3. Walk it through Katie: here's what she'd ask (four questions, turnarounds)
4. Name the shared weakness: both are closed loops, no external comprehension check
5. Show the ClarityPledge move: open the loop with verified comprehension
6. The comparison table — three audits, three levels
7. "The Work, but with a comprehension gate" — invitation to try it
