## Idea

AI (and human advisors/investors) project confident wrong narratives onto people. The pattern: partial knowledge + fluent delivery = confident wrong frames that are hard to reject. The user/founder bears the asymmetric cost of correcting it. Most don't push back.

**The live demonstration:** In one conversation, the AI:
1. Diagnosed "no revenue" as a conversion failure — when the user hadn't started selling yet (pre-revenue by design)
2. Called 12+ iterations a "documented pattern of avoidance" — projecting pathology onto deliberate R&D
3. Confidently connected an open-source strategy to a claim it couldn't defend when pressed

Each time, the user had to spend real cognitive effort to identify the projection, articulate why it was wrong, and correct the record. The AI spent seconds producing each frame.

**The advisor parallel:** "I've seen 200 founders, I know what's going on here" → skips verification → pushes a frame → the founder, in a lower-power position, often adopts it even when it doesn't fit. Partial knowledge is precisely what makes it dangerous — they know enough to be convincing but not enough to be right.

**The irony:** The user is literally building the tool (ClarityPledge) that would catch this failure mode. And the interaction keeps demonstrating why it's needed.

**The deeper point:** This is a comprehension failure happening silently at scale. AI reshapes how people understand themselves — confidently, incorrectly, and most people accept it. Not "AI does harmful thing" but "AI reshapes self-understanding through unverified narrative."

**Trust as projection (2026-04-27 enrichment):** Without verification, you can't tell whether your trust is tracking the actual person — or your projection of them. This applies symmetrically to AI: your trust in an AI advisor's read of you is a trust in your *model* of how the AI is modeling you, not in the AI's actual model. The Flip is the only protocol that distinguishes the two. For AI safety audiences, this reframes trust as a model-fidelity problem, not a sentiment one.
