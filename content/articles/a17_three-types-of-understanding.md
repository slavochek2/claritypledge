# Three Types of Understanding — Why "I Get It" Means Three Different Things

## Strategic Intent

**Serves:** Accessible entry point for the rate-asymmetry argument. Entry-level audience — not the LessWrong mechanism piece (that's a9). Linked from a9 Point 1 once a17 ships.

**Audience:** General readers curious about communication and understanding. Professionals who work in teams, partnerships, or coaching.

**Positioning:** Taxonomy article — names three types of understanding that collapse into one phrase ("I get it") and shows why that collapse creates structural verification failures.

## Three Types

1. **Declarative understanding** — "I know the fact." Knowing that Paris is the capital of France. Verifiable by quiz.

2. **Procedural understanding** — "I can apply it." Knowing how to navigate Paris. Verifiable by doing.

3. **Cognitive/relational understanding** — "I know you know I know." Understanding what someone MEANS, with enough fidelity that they would agree you understood. Not verifiable by quiz or demonstration alone — only by the speaker confirming your paraphrase.

## Core Argument

Most communication assumes type 1 is sufficient when the conversation requires type 3. The verification cost difference is the hidden mechanism behind most misalignment.

Type 3 is expensive specifically because the cognitive channel has no native signal — you cannot observe another person's mental model directly. The protocol (paraphrase + confirm) closes this gap by adding the missing signal.

## Connection to H-AgreementSubstitution

When type-3 verification is absent, agreement becomes the only ambiguity-resolving signal available. The result: what looks like a disagreement about positions is often a failure of type-3 verification upstream.
