# Common Ground vs Common Knowledge vs Common Language

## Idea

Three terms get used interchangeably in conversations about understanding, but they describe distinct constructs at different layers. **Common ground** (Clark, linguistic pragmatics) is the assumed shared context two speakers operate from. **Common knowledge** (Lewis/Aumann, game theory) is the recursive structure "I know that you know that I know..." that lets coordination work. **Common language** is high-bandwidth shared vocabulary — a surface signal that masks whether the underlying mutual-knowledge structure actually holds. ClarityPledge's contribution is to operationalize the verification step that separates them: shared vocabulary doesn't imply common ground, and common ground doesn't imply common knowledge. The Clarity Flip is the protocol that promotes one to the next — what ClarityPledge calls *recursive understanding*.

This article disambiguates the three for a rationalist/EA audience and shows where each existing literature stops short of instrumentation. It also frames the meta-skill positioning: the product is not "learn the ClarityPledge protocol" but "learn the meta-skill of producing verified mutual understanding with anyone — across any disagreement, any cultural difference, any topic."

## Enrichment (2026-05-24): Pinker's dyadic blindspot

Source: Conversation "Pinker vs Slava on common knowledge — leader-selection extension"
Applied to: a-spec body

A refinement for the "where each literature stops short" thread. Pinker's common-knowledge account is **dyadic** — it theorizes the strategic speaker managing one listener's ambiguity. It undertheorizes the actor who must generate common knowledge *for a population* (a leader shaping what a whole side takes as shared red lines/intentions). His evidence base is concentrated in dyadic strategic interactions (innuendo, bribes, propositions); less developed on multi-party repeated games. Naming this locates exactly where the instrumentation gap sits: Clark gives common ground, Lewis/Aumann give the formal recursive structure, Pinker gives the dyadic strategic logic — and none provides the *verification protocol* that promotes shared belief to common belief, at either the dyad or the population scale.
