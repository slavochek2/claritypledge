# Common Ground vs Common Knowledge vs Common Language

## Idea

Three terms get used interchangeably in conversations about understanding, but they describe distinct constructs at different layers. **Common ground** (Clark, linguistic pragmatics) is the assumed shared context two speakers operate from. **Common knowledge** (Lewis/Aumann, game theory) is the recursive structure "I know that you know that I know..." that lets coordination work. **Common language** is high-bandwidth shared vocabulary — a surface signal that masks whether the underlying mutual-knowledge structure actually holds. ClarityPledge's contribution is to operationalize the verification step that separates them: shared vocabulary doesn't imply common ground, and common ground doesn't imply common knowledge. The Clarity Flip is the protocol that promotes one to the next.

This article disambiguates the three for a rationalist/EA audience and shows where each existing literature stops short of instrumentation. It also frames the meta-skill positioning: the product is not "learn the ClarityPledge protocol" but "learn the meta-skill of producing verified mutual understanding with anyone — across any disagreement, any cultural difference, any topic."
