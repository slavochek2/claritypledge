# What a Badge Actually Measures — Capacity, Not Trait

## Idea

Critics of any integrity-adjacent measurement reach for the same objection: "you can't measure integrity." The objection is right about the trait and wrong about the protocol. ClarityPledge's badge does not certify a person's integrity — it certifies behavioral adherence inside a specific protocol: that this person, on this story, demonstrated paraphrase-verified comprehension at the depth the speaker accepted. Falsifiable. Observable. Bounded.

Once you separate what the badge measures (capacity to uphold the verification standard in a session) from what readers infer (trait-level epistemic trustworthiness), you can defend the instrument without overclaiming. Inter-rater reliability, test-retest, and discriminant validity are provable today. Predictive validity for trait-level integrity needs longitudinal data. This article is the answer to "isn't this just measuring whether people are nice in a conversation?"
