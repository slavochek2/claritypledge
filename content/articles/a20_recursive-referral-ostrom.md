# Recursive Referral as Commons Governance

## Idea

The badging propagation rule — Level N is "brought N people of Level N-1 who completed the Flip with you" — is structurally a commons governance mechanism, not a marketing funnel. Each ancestor's status depends on the verification integrity of their entire downstream tree, which makes the network self-policing. Ostrom's design principles for governing the commons (clear boundaries, monitoring, graduated sanctions, nested enterprises) map onto this directly: the protocol IS the boundary, the Flip IS the monitoring, badge revocation IS the sanction, and Levels nest.

This frames ClarityPledge as institutional commons infrastructure rather than a proprietary product — coherent with the open-source license and with the founder's "intelligence infrastructure for human understanding" thesis. It also explains why volume-driven growth corrupts the instrument: any sale that bypasses the verification step degrades the lineage of every node downstream.
