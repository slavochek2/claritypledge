# When "Heads I Win, Tails You Lose" Dresses as Self-Knowledge

## Idea

Somatic therapy makes a Popper-shaped claim that fails Popper's test: "feeling somatic pain confirms the theory; not feeling it = dissociation, which also confirms the theory." Every observation supports the framework. There is no outcome that would falsify it.

This is the structural shape Popper called out as the boundary between science and pseudoscience — and it's the same shape that hides inside many self-knowledge claims. The article uses somatic therapy as the worked example, then generalizes: any theory of inner states where absence of the predicted experience is *also* explained by the theory has the same problem.

**Distinct from a21 (Popper → negative-utilitarian drift):** a21 is about the moral gravity of fallibilism; this is about falsifiability as a diagnostic tool against unfalsifiable self-knowledge claims.

## Why article-worthy

- Clean Popper case study with a real practitioner community (not just philosophical hypothetical).
- Audience-bridging: rationalists already trust falsifiability; therapy-curious readers get a new frame for evaluating their practices.
- Connects to ClarityPledge via the verification principle: the same way "I felt understood" doesn't verify understanding, "I felt something somatic" doesn't verify the somatic theory.
