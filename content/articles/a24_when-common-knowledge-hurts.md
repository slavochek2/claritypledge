# When Common Knowledge Hurts — and When It Doesn't

## Idea

Pinker's body of work (*The Stuff of Thought*; "The Logic of Indirect Speech," PNAS 2008; *When Everyone Knows That Everyone Knows*, 2025) makes a precise claim that is easy to over-read: when interests or values diverge, **common knowledge is irreversible and removes the face-saving equilibrium** that depends on plausible deniability. Indirect speech preserves ambiguity precisely because direct speech would force a costly recategorization of the relationship.

ClarityPledge's thesis appears to contradict this: even across value differences, **recursive understanding helps** — knowing that the other knows your values beats not knowing. This article shows the apparent contradiction is not one. The two claims operate on different premises and the resolution sharpens both.

## The core move — capacity vs choice

This is the article's original contribution and the reason it stands alone rather than folding into a10 or a18.

- **Pinker's frame** assumes *capable strategic actors* choosing **when** to deploy common-knowledge creation. It is a strategy-selection argument: sometimes you rationally choose ambiguity.
- **The ClarityPledge frame** is about the **capacity floor**: what if an agent *cannot* mentalize across the difference at all? You cannot choose to deploy a capacity you lack. A person stuck in their default cognition cannot even *select* productive ambiguity.

Pinker is right about strategy given capacity. The Flip thesis is about the precondition Pinker assumes. Not a contradiction — a different layer.

## Deciding-variables table (the spine of the piece)

When does building common knowledge of a value gap help vs hurt?

| Variable | Favors avoiding CK (Pinker) | Favors building CK (Flip) |
|---|---|---|
| Time horizon | Short, transactional | Long, repeated |
| Asymmetry | One side benefits from ambiguity | Both bear the cost of pretending |
| Reversibility of relationship | Hard to exit, must coexist | Exit is a real option |
| Stakes of the value gap | Low, won't recur | High, surfaces under stress |
| Content type | Specific actions / propositions | Underlying values / principles |
| Face-saving alternative | Silence works | Silence breeds resentment |

Synthesis: common knowledge of *specific propositions* in *short-horizon* interactions where *ambiguity is load-bearing* tends to destabilize (Pinker is right). Common knowledge of *underlying values* in *long-horizon* relationships where *both parties bear the cost of pretending* tends to stabilize (the Flip is right).

## What the Flip thesis must concede

- The **irreversibility cost** is real: even when recursive understanding is net-positive long-term, the *transition* through forced recategorization can rupture the relationship before the better equilibrium is reached.
- Common knowledge can lock in the *worse* party's position ("you knew this about me — why complain now?").
- The article should NOT claim recursive understanding always helps. It should claim: in the domains ClarityPledge serves (co-founders, partnerships, long-horizon high-stakes), the variables line up on the build-CK side.

## Pinker's blindspot worth naming

Pinker's frame is dyadic and undertheorizes the actor who must generate common knowledge *for a population* (the leader case — see a25). His evidence base is concentrated in dyadic strategic interactions (innuendo, bribes, propositions). Less developed on multi-party repeated games. Naming this is the positioning move, not an attack.
