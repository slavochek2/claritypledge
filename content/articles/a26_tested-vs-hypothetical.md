# What's Tested, What's Hypothetical — Inside ClarityPledge's Claims

## Idea

Most product narratives present their full thesis as established. ClarityPledge's differentiator is the opposite move: **publicly grade our own claims by evidence state.** This is a credibility asset, not a liability — it demonstrates the calibration the product is about. The article is the falsification discipline turned outward.

This is the trust-building counterpart to the assertive pieces (a9, a24, a25). It says: here is exactly which load-bearing claims are proven, which are structurally supported, and which are untested bets.

## The grading table (spine of the piece)

| Claim | Evidence state |
|---|---|
| Common knowledge changes equilibria in mixed-motive games | **Proven** (Schelling, Aumann, formal game theory) |
| Speakers overestimate being understood (72% believed vs 61% actual) | **Measured** (Keysar & Henly 2002) |
| Closeness doesn't improve accuracy | **Measured** (Savitsky et al. 2011) |
| Perspective-*taking* instructions fail to improve accuracy | **Replicated** (Eyal, Steffel & Epley 2018 — 25 experiments, zero benefit) |
| Illusion of transparency / false consensus / curse of knowledge are real and large | **Robustly replicated** (Gilovich 1998; Ross 1977; Camerer et al. 1989) |
| Self-assessed comprehension barely tracks actual (reading, r=0.178) | **Measured** (Yang et al. 2023, N=15,889) |
| Listener-side conversational calibration | **Unmeasured** — the construct ClarityPledge claims to instrument |
| The Flip lifts the recursive-belief floor in live sessions | **Untested** — needs workshop data |
| Leader mentalizing capacity predicts collective outcomes | **Structural + historical only**, no controlled test (see a25) |
| Badge predicts trait-level epistemic trustworthiness | **Untested** — needs longitudinal data (see a19) |

## Why publish this

- **Naive realism / bias blind spot** (Pronin): everyone thinks their own claims are "just true." Grading our own is the rare disconfirming signal that we mean the calibration thesis.
- It pre-empts the strongest critic move ("you're overclaiming") by doing the critic's job first, in public.
- It models the exact behavior the product sells: distinguishing verified from felt.

## What this is NOT

Not hedging, not a disclaimer dump. Each row names *what would move it* to "tested." The article is a roadmap of the experiments that would settle each open claim — which is itself a research agenda.

## Enrichment (2026-06-26)
Source: "Survivorship bias in founder success stories" (2026-06-26)
Applied to: a-spec body

A time-indexing addition to the tested/hypothetical line: the **PMF inflection point** is where the control boundary shifts. Pre-PMF, nearly all outcomes are *bets* (the knowledge that makes the transformation possible doesn't exist yet); post-PMF, *some* become genuinely forecastable. So "refusing to commit to a revenue date" pre-PMF isn't temperamental caution — it's epistemically correct and *time-indexed to current knowledge*. The honest claim isn't "we don't forecast"; it's "this is a bet now, and the day it becomes a forecast is itself an input we control (by building the conversion mechanism)." Strengthens a26's core move — grading our own claims by their epistemic status — by adding *when* a hypothetical becomes legitimately testable.
