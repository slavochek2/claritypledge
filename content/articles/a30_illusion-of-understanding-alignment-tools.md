# The Illusion of Understanding — Why Alignment Tools Burn Months Confusing "We Don't Understand" With "We Don't Agree"

> Working title — not final. `[FOUNDER DECISION: title]`. Alternatives if a sharper hook emerges: "Two Gaps, One Frame" · "Which Gap Is Blocking You?" · "The Agile Trap."

## The idea

Most "alignment" tools conflate two structurally different failures — *"we don't understand each other"* (a meaning gap) and *"we don't agree"* (a validity disagreement) — and burn months treating one as the other. The core innovation of the protocol is that it tells a team **which** gap is blocking them right now. Meaning gaps are reconcilable with effort; validity disagreements may not be — and you can only carry a disagreement honestly once you've ruled out that it was a misunderstanding in disguise.

## The objection that strengthens it

A sharp founder says: *"I don't need us to agree — I need us to disagree and commit."* That's the agile orthodoxy, and it sounds like a counter. It isn't. "Disagree and commit" presupposes you can tell a real disagreement from a misunderstanding — which is exactly the thing teams can't do without verification. You can't commit to a disagreement you haven't confirmed is one. (Pre-empt this objection in the article and on the landing-page Q&A.)
