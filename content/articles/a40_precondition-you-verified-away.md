# The Precondition You Verified Away

> Working title — not final. `[FOUNDER DECISION: title]`.

## The idea

A verification instrument that treats the *affective* layer as noise to strip away will read garbage. In a cold or status-threatening room, people round their comprehension self-estimates **up** to save face — so the Min bound becomes a lie and the instrument measures nothing real. Affective safety is not the soft opposite of rigor; it is the **integrity precondition** for an honest Min estimate. "Cold but correct" doesn't just feel bad — it corrupts the input.

The twist (and why this is article-worthy, not just a facilitation tip): the affective layer was **already instrumented and we hadn't named it**. The facilitator's "how important is it right now that the other agrees?" question, asked before and after the paraphrase round, measures the affective shift directly. The article makes the invisible visible: the safety floor we thought was outside the instrument is the thing the instrument was quietly already reading — and the honesty of every comprehension number depends on it.

Counter-foil from the source conversation: an operator (Justin Lee's site) who manufactures a *real* psychological state — men willing to say the embarrassing true thing — on top of a *false* map. The lesson isn't "add warmth"; it's de-bullshit the affective layer without eliminating it, because eliminating it removes the condition under which the Min is honest.

## Arc

ARC-2 (The Invisible Made Visible) — the affective safety floor was already measured by the agreement-importance metric but unrecognized as such. Secondary ARC-4: the unquestioned assumption that the affective layer is noise turns out to be the instrument's integrity condition.
