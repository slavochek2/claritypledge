# Which World Is This Claim In? The Fact/Meaning Splitter

## Idea

Most stuck disagreements are one fight that is secretly two. Every claim sits in one of
three camps, sorted by a single test — **"is our agreement the truthmaker, or just evidence
of one?"**:

- **Camp A — Evidential (Fact / "the world settles it").** Agreement is *evidence* of an
  external truth; reality is the arbiter. "The bridge holds." Verify against the world.
- **Camp B — Constitutive (Meaning / "we settle it").** Agreement *is* the truthmaker; there
  is no external arbiter, only the other mind's confirmation. "Did I understand what you
  meant?" Verify against the person (paraphrase-and-confirm).
- **Camp C — Fused.** The real, interesting claims — where a checkable strand and an only-we
  strand are tangled in one sentence. The "aha" is: *your one fight is actually two fights.*

The core move is not sorting into a binary bucket — it's **splitting** (disentangling) a
Camp-C claim into its evidential strand (checkable against the world) and its constitutive
strand (checkable only against each other). This is the same operation ClarityPledge's format
already runs under a different name: **story vs point IS the Camp-C → (A, B) split.**

Caveat to keep (do not sand off): the split is rarely clean even within a single sentence —
sell it as *disentangling*, not *separating*. Camp A is also better read as
"settled-enough-that-nobody's-checking" than as a permanently terminal state — a challenge
can de-harden A back to B.

## Why it matters (three uses, all surfaced 2026-07)

1. **Content / framework.** A clean, teachable taxonomy that makes an invisible confusion
   (conflating *agreement* with *understanding*) nameable and checkable. ARC-2.
2. **Core product infrastructure (feature — see Feature note).** The splitter is the operation
   that generates points (Camp A, checkable positions) from stories (Camp B, meaning-laden) —
   "not just a lead magnet, core infrastructure for creating points and stories" (founder marker).
3. **Lead magnet (feature).** An interactive "drop in a claim → see which world it's in" page —
   but built as a *splitter* (pulls apart the checkable strand vs the only-we strand), not a
   binary sorter, because the interesting real claims are Camp C.

## The underlying engine: meaning hardens into fact via shared consequence

Meaning starts in Camp B (unshared, needs verification) and **hardens into Camp A via repeated
shared consequence** — shared consequence is the referee. Where an evolved consequence-arbiter
*fails* is exactly the three early-adopter criteria (see a29 / lean-canvas): **fuzzy intent**
(too ambiguous to arbitrate), **delayed feedback** (too late to arbitrate), **concentrated
stakes** (too costly to arbitrate by trial). Those three are not an ad-hoc marketing heuristic —
they are the only three ways a consequence-arbiter can fail. This is the mechanistic *why*
behind the rate-asymmetry thesis.
