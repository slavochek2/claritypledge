# The Gap You Don't Know You Have — Why Every Method for Surfacing It Needs a Symptom First

> Working title — `[FOUNDER DECISION: title]`.

## Arc
ARC-1 (Tested and Tightened) — the bold claim ("nobody trains surfacing unknown unknowns") is stated, tested against three revered methods, the overclaim is exposed, and the tightened, defensible version is offered with the weakness named.

## Source
Conversations: "Sales training business model concerns" (2026-07-09) and "Building an alignment coaching business" (2026-07-11). The founder reached the sharpest name for the product yet — "a skill for finding the gap you don't know you have" — and then stress-tested it live against Socratic method, therapy, and double-crux.

## The idea
Distinguish two kinds of unknown unknown: the gap **you** can't see but a second mind can, and the gap **neither** party can see. Almost every existing method works the first kind. The deadliest case is the second: **the illusion of agreement** — two people who think they already understand each other, with no conflict and no felt confusion to flag a problem. Nothing signals it, so nothing investigates it.

Walk the three revered methods and show each needs a **pre-existing symptom** to fire:
- **Socratic method** — forces articulation; surfaces contradictions *already latent in one head*. It's a catalyst, not a second source of content. It cannot surface what's simply absent from your model. Precondition: the gap must already be latent in one head.
- **Therapy** (good therapy) — surfaces unknowns you're *motivated not to see*; mechanism is safety + a mirror lowering a defense. Specialty is the *emotionally* guarded unknown, not the cognitively unarticulated one. Precondition: an emotional defense to lower.
- **Double-crux** — the closest prior art; bilateral. Reveals the unknown unknown at the moment two people discover their cruxes are about *different variables entirely*. But it presupposes a **stated disagreement** as raw material. Precondition: an open disagreement already on the table.

The defensible synthesis: each of these requires a pre-existing symptom (a stated belief, a felt defense, an open disagreement). **Bilateral paraphrase is the only one that generates the signal** — it manufactures the friction by making each person reconstruct the other and checks the delta, so it attacks the illusion-of-agreement case, which has no symptom to start from.

## Honesty fix (the tightening — do not ship the overclaim)
Do **not** ship "nobody trains that" or "nobody surfaces unknown unknowns." Socratic method, good therapy, and double-crux (which the rationalist/EA audience already runs) all touch unknown unknowns. The claim that survives is narrower and stronger: *nobody does it **bilaterally and verified** — as a repeatable two-person protocol with a measurable flip — for the symptomless illusion-of-agreement case.* A reader who knows these methods rejects the broad claim on sight; the narrow one holds.

## Why it's the sharper name (not a relabel)
Earlier framings the founder reached for under pressure — "truth-based selling," "alignment training" — were relabels (a nicer word for a bad feeling). This one is not: it falls out of the actual mechanism. Paraphrase *is* how you surface a gap you couldn't see — the other person says back what they think you meant, and the delta is the unknown unknown made visible. The engine, not a coat of paint.

## Why sales felt like a lie (the channel-capture connection)
Standard sales works the *known* objection — the gap the buyer can already feel — and optimizes for *their yes*. This protocol works the gap neither party knew was there and is willing to *lower* the demand for agreement. Different target entirely. That's why packaging it as sales training kept feeling dishonest: you'd be selling the surfacing of hidden gaps to buyers who only want to close visible ones.
