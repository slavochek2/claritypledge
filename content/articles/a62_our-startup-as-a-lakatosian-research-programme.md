# Our Startup Is a Lakatosian Research Programme

**Arc:** the company's own epistemics applied to the company — the product verifies understanding between two people; Lakatos's framework does the same job one level up, for the relationship between a founder and their own strategy.
**Source conversation:** Lakatos framing session (2026-08-03). Infrastructure counterpart: `features/p1026_research_programme_rigor_layer.md`.

## Idea

Lean startup tells you to falsify hypotheses. It does not tell you what to do when the falsifications keep arriving and you keep pivoting — is that learning, or thrashing? Popper alone can't answer: every individual pivot is impeccably falsificationist. Lakatos can, because his unit of appraisal is not the hypothesis but the *programme*: a protected **hard core**, a **protective belt** of auxiliary hypotheses that absorbs refutations (segments, channels, pricing, entry mechanisms), and a verdict — **progressive** if belt adjustments predict novel facts that get corroborated, **degenerating** if each adjustment is ad-hoc, accommodating only the anomaly that killed its predecessor.

The confession that gives the article its spine: we had run this structure implicitly for months — our docs literally say "a failed hypothesis triggers mechanism transformation, not project abandonment," which *is* the negative heuristic — while our progress metric ("hypotheses falsified per unit time") was measuring activity, not progress. A degenerating programme maximizes that metric beautifully.

## The moves worth teaching

1. **Write the core down, then attack it.** Our first draft of the hard core failed adversarial review: three of five elements were belt hypotheses in costume (one had a published falsifier and two revisions — a protected commitment cannot also be scheduled for a filter test), and the two commitments we had never once questioned (the speaker is the sole arbiter of being-understood; the unit is a human relationship) appeared nowhere, precisely *because* they had never been questioned. The core you'd write from memory is the current belt frozen in time; the real core is found by asking what survived every pivot.
2. **Falsifiers and novel predictions are different disciplines.** A falsifier protects you from dogmatism. A novel-prediction requirement ("what does this framing predict that its predecessor didn't — stated before the test?") protects you from degeneration. We had exhaustive falsifiers on every hypothesis and novel predictions on none.
3. **You abandon a programme for a rival, not for anomalies.** Naming the rival programmes (for us: affect-first listening, coordination tooling, AI-mediated alignment, and the null programme — "affinity trust is adequate") turns "should we quit?" from a mood into a comparison.
4. **Context of discovery vs context of justification.** Successor framings do not arrive on schedule; ours have historically arrived only *after* the falsifying evidence landed, not before. Lakatos makes that respectable: ideas may come from anywhere — the rigor applies at adoption (document the novel content the successor adds), not at conception.
5. **Lakatos has a known hole — patch it yourself.** He gives no rational moment to quit (Feyerabend's objection): a degenerating programme can always promise recovery. A founder can self-impose what the methodology can't: a resource-bounded stopping rule — "if no novel prediction is corroborated within N tests, the pivot cadence itself becomes the anomaly."

## The recursive kicker

The product is a Lakatosian instrument for dyads: verified comprehension locates whether a disagreement hits your counterpart's core or their belt — most fights are belt-fights mistaken for core-fights. And the article demonstrates the practice it describes: the company submitting its own strategy to the protocol it sells.
