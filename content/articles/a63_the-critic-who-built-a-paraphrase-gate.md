# The Critic Who Built a Paraphrase Gate

> Working title — `[FOUNDER DECISION: title]`. Alternatives: "Everyone Cites the 1998 Paper. Nobody Cites the 2019 Worksheet." · "Convergence, Not Correction."

## Arc

ARC-4 (The Assumption Nobody Questioned) — the audience believes a famous finding settled a question. The person who produced that finding has since built the thing it supposedly refuted. That convergence is the lede. The correction to the famous finding is a supporting section, not the headline.

## Why the framing is inverted — read this before drafting

The first version of this article led with *"the most-cited attack on paraphrasing was never a test of paraphrasing."* That version was killed in adversarial review, for three reasons that should keep it dead:

1. **Strategic.** `lean-canvas.md:676` defines a reachable segment partly *by Gottman affiliation* ("relational / couples / EFT-Gottman … therapists"), and `decisions.md` names our own two-brand revenue architecture "the Gottman split." A hostile reader needs one grep of this public repo to write *"he copied the business model and calls the flagship paper's reasoning a sin."*
2. **Legal.** The author publishes from Germany, where for a **factual assertion** about a named living person the burden of proving truth sits with the publisher (§186 StGB, §823/1004 BGB). A *Unterlassungsanspruch* needs no damages and no malice. Every claim about what a paper contains or omits is a factual assertion.
3. **Editorial — the decisive one.** Convergence is simply the better piece. "The most-cited critic of active listening built a paraphrase gate" is a stronger, stranger, more useful sentence than "the critic over-reached," and it lets every concession below land as candour rather than defence.

**Hard rule for the draft: no sentence may allege that anyone concealed, buried, or selectively cited anything.** Attacking a published inference is fair and safe. Impugning scholarly integrity is neither. Three phrasings from the killed draft are banned by name: "the exact sin the article accuses the paper of", "kept it in-house", "the citation is incomplete."

## Move 1 — The convergence (the lede)

The Gottman Method's current conflict intervention, the **Gottman-Rapoport blueprint**, holds:

> "No one can engage in persuasion until both of you can state your partner's position to your partner's satisfaction."

The listener restates content, names feelings, asks clarifying questions. Persuasion is postponed until the *speaker* signs off. That is a paraphrase gate with the speaker as arbiter — the same architecture this project arrived at from a completely different direction (see `docs/definitions.md` §Stories vs Points, referent locus).

**Carry the caveat in the same breath:** Gottman-Rapoport is training material, not a trial. No component RCT exists. It is prior art in *practice*, never evidence of efficacy.

## Move 2 — What the 1998 paper actually found (supporting, not headline)

Gottman, Coan, Carrère & Swanson (1998) followed 130 newlyweds for six years. Nobody was trained; nobody was randomized. The couples had a **15-minute lab conflict discussion** on topics selected with an experimenter, and seven process models were tested against divorce and satisfaction — active listening among them.

Three facts, all from the primary, all quotable:

- Active-listening sequences were **"totaling approximately 4 seconds out of 900"** (p.13) — about **0.44%** of the discussion, measured in seconds of airtime. *(The "4.4%" that circulates in secondary sources is a decimal-shift error. Do not repeat it. This article's own first draft carried it.)*
- The paper does recommend that marital therapy **"abandon the active listening model"** (p.20). That is its language.
- And the sentence almost nobody quotes, from the authors themselves (p.17): the validation code was applied **"exclusively for what Duncan and Fiske (1977) had called 'listener backchannels'"** — brief vocalizations, head nods, facial movements.

That last one is the whole section. **The study's own authors report that what they coded was nodding, not paraphrasing.** Whatever the finding establishes, it is not about the mechanic Gottman-Rapoport would later gate persuasion on. This is a construct point, made in the original authors' words — no accusation required, and none should be added.

The field's response is public record and can be cited neutrally: Stanley, Bradbury & Markman (2000) disputed the leap from observation to intervention in the same journal; Gottman replied; Hafen & Crane (2003) wrote it up as unresolved.

**Accuracy note for the draft:** the Munich/Hahlweg study is **not cited in the 1998 paper** — the reference list runs Guerney → Heavey with no Hahlweg-authored entry. Munich appears in the 2000 reply. Get the locator right or omit the point.

## Move 3 — The harder problem, and the real payload

From Bradbury & Bodenmann (2020), *Annual Review of Clinical Psychology* — quote with the inline citation intact, because the review is summarising someone else's primary result and dropping that would be the exact move this article declines to make:

> "changes in observed behavior do not mediate treatment effects on satisfaction (e.g., Williamson et al. 2016)."

> "consistent, lasting, and consequential changes in couple communication have not been documented."

Programs help. Whether they help *through* communication is not established. Lavner, Karney & Bradbury (2016) found both causal arrows weak — good communication looks about as much like a consequence of a good relationship as a cause.

**State the floor as well as the ceiling.** Hawkins et al. (2008) report d ≈ .43–.45 on communication skills and .30–.36 on relationship quality. The same review reports d ≈ 0.09 in a 6,298-couple trial, and a study in which skills-trained newlyweds did not outperform couples simply told to watch and discuss movies. Citing only the meta-analytic ceiling would be selective in precisely the way this article is about.

## What the article concedes about CP

Non-negotiable. Without these it is marketing.

- CP's theory of change is a mediation claim, and CP has not run a mediation test.
- The pilot as currently sized cannot run one — n≈10 is badly underpowered for an indirect-effect test. What it can do is validate an accuracy measure and size a real study.
- CP's own mediator measure is currently a participant self-report collected by the vendor. That is the "seller as judge" problem, and naming an independent coder is a precondition, not a detail.
- The strongest null — affinity-based trust is adequate for most dyads — is not refuted by CP's own field data. See the true-null demo.

## What it must NOT concede — the scope discipline

The couples failure is *observer-coded behavior → self-reported satisfaction over multi-year lags*. CP's mediator is speaker-adjudicated paraphrase accuracy; its outcome is decision quality over weeks. **The design lesson transfers. The pessimism about whether the mediator is measurable at all does not.** An earlier draft conceded this and was wrong to — and the same discipline forbids importing the couples finding onto healthcare teach-back, where the outcome evidence is strong and the pathway is simply untested.

## The closing turn

Two protocols, arrived at independently, in different decades, for different rooms, converge on the same gate: **restate it to the other person's satisfaction before you proceed.** A third — closed-loop readback in aviation and medicine — got there earlier than either, in the work domain, with hard outcome measures.

What none of them has is evidence about *why* it works. That is the open problem, it is stated as such by the field's own review (Future Issue #4: "Can we conduct controlled experiments that test proposed mediators of treatment effects?"), and it is the honest description of what this project should do next.
