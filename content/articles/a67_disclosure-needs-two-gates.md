# Disclosure Needs Two Gates

> Working title — `[FOUNDER DECISION: title]`. Alternatives: "Legibility and Safety Are Different Gates" · "Why I Kept Installing the Norm in Rooms That Couldn't Hold It."

## Arc

**ARC-4 (The Assumption Nobody Questioned)** — a widely accepted premise (psychological safety explains who speaks up) is shown to have a hidden dependency (it governs *consequence*, never *comprehensibility*), the consequences of the gap are traced, and the fix is proposed.

Founder-flagged for this treatment: *"this is important — I thought I could normalize revealing gaps and that's OK, but it's true that competence also updates quietly."*

## The claim

Disclosure requires **two independent gates**, and both must open:

1. **Legibility** — will they understand me if I say it?
2. **Safety** — what happens to me once they do?

The protocol raises the first. Edmondson's construct governs the second. Nothing in the project's docs had separated them, and that conflation explains a specific repeated failure: **installing the disclosure norm in a low-safety team produces compliance theatre** — the number gets said without being meant, which shows up as high stated comprehension with unchanged behavior.

The founder's own diagnosis in the source conversation, unprompted: *"maybe that was my biggest gap — I tried to install the norm, but I can't, for teams with low psychological safety."*

## The correction to the founder's own model — this is the load-bearing part

The working assumption was: **normalize the disclosure and the cost disappears.** If admitting a gap is expected rather than punished, nobody needs to save face.

That is wrong, and the mechanism is Pinker's, from inside the project's own intellectual lineage — which makes it an internal inconsistency rather than an outside objection.

**Pinker's mechanism does not require punishment.** It requires only that status models get *updated*. In a fully benign group with zero sanction, common knowledge of my gap still forces everyone to revise their competence estimate of me. That is not punishment; it is information, and you cannot norm it away. **You can normalize the disclosure without normalizing the inference.**

Worse: common knowledge is irreversible. Once we both know that we both know, the graceful path — where I quietly go figure it out and nobody has to update anything — is foreclosed. Deniability protects exactly that path, and the protocol manufactures common knowledge *deliberately*.

**So the tension is structural. You can price it. You cannot eliminate it.**

## Two consequences the author did not see coming

**1. The competence update is partly a feature.** The immediate reading is "this is a cost." But a team that knows who actually knows what routes questions correctly and does not overtrust. That is transactive-memory formation (Wegner; Lewis's *credibility* dimension), and the project's own listening-calibration ledger is literally a credibility measure with a number attached. The cost is not *exposure* — it is **asymmetric** exposure.

**2. Therefore the binding constraint on a clarity organization's size is reciprocity closure, not bandwidth.** In a small repeated group everyone reveals eventually and the ledger balances: whoever reveals a gap today receives one tomorrow. Above some *n*, some members never reciprocate, and revelation becomes a one-way status transfer. **That is a testable ceiling on membership size**, and it comes from a different direction than any capacity argument.

## The sequencing correction — dose, not gate

The obvious inference from "safety must open too" is: measure psychological safety, fix it, *then* install the norm. That is too clean.

Nembhard and Edmondson's work suggests safety is *built by* small acts of inviting and appreciating contribution — which is what the protocol is. So the relationship is plausibly bidirectional: a low-stakes version installs safety, which permits a higher-stakes version. The failure mode is not *norm before safety*; it is **high-stakes norm before any safety**.

**Dose, not gate** — which means the founder does not have to wait.

And the fix was already built: the facilitator-fails-first mechanic (the highest-status person says the first low number, about themselves) *is* Edmondson's leader-fallibility-modeling antecedent. It had simply never been connected to the sequencing problem. See `docs/hypotheses.md` H-NormFlip and `content/articles/a51`.

## Where the novelty actually sits — do not overclaim

Morrison's voice literature already splits **voice safety** (is it safe to speak) from **voice efficacy** (will speaking change anything). Legibility is neither — it is about being *comprehended*, not about consequence or impact. That gap looks real, but it needs a proper literature check before anything is built on it.

Second overclaim to avoid: the transparency conditional as originally stated — *"you cannot be transparent with people you expect to misunderstand you"* — is false as written. People confess to strangers, therapists, and anonymous forums precisely *because* no ongoing model is at stake. The defensible version is about **motivation, not capability**: if I expect the gap cannot be bridged, I am less motivated to try. Mayer's trust model puts benevolence and integrity ahead of ability for the same reason, and the competent rival who understands you perfectly is exactly who you are most guarded with.

## The closing turn

The project has been selling a legibility instrument into rooms whose problem was safety, and reading the resulting silence as a failure of the instrument.

Two gates. The product opens one of them. Knowing which one is the difference between a diagnostic and a disappointment — and it changes what gets sold, what gets measured first, and how big a clarity organization can get before its own ledger stops balancing.

## Guards

- **Do not present the two-gate split as established.** It is a reframe from one conversation, checked against Morrison only from memory.
- **Do not read the size ceiling as a number.** No *n* is derivable from anything currently in hand; the claim is that the ceiling exists and is set by reciprocity, not that it is at 8 or 30.
- **Do not use the competence-updating point to argue disclosure is bad.** The article's own move is that the update is partly desirable — say so before naming the cost, or it reads as an argument against the product.
