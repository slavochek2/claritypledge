# The Observable of Last Resort

> Working title — `[FOUNDER DECISION: title]`. Alternatives: "Three Dials, Not One Problem" · "What You Check When You Cannot Check the Work."

## Arc

**ARC-1 (Tested and Tightened)** — a claim is stated boldly, each link is tested under pressure, the weak links are exposed and patched, and the tightened version is offered with its vulnerability named. The distinguishing feature here is that the source conversation ran the tightening **six times** on the record, so the article can show the versions rather than assert that rigor happened.

## The claim chain (v6, as it stood at the end of the conversation)

Whether a coordination problem is solvable is a function of three properties, not one:

- **observability** — can contributions be seen?
- **specifiability** — can the load-bearing terms be pinned down well enough to arbitrate?
- **explanatory divergence** — do the parties hold different causal stories that each fully explain the same outcome?

Where contributions are **uncheckable**, verified comprehension is **the observable of last resort** — the only thing left that can be inspected when the work itself cannot be.

## Why this is not a restatement of the Popper Gap

The conversation's sharpest move was separating two claims that had been travelling as one:

- the **assurance** claim — verification substitutes for an absent arbiter;
- the **Popper Gap** claim — criticism as a stopping rule presupposes the criticism was understood.

They are **siblings, not the same claim**. An article that keeps conflating them cannot state either precisely. Naming the split is the payload.

## The founder-flagged extension: groups as units

Marked `[/cp articl3? focs update?]` in the source:

> "a group that doesn't verify internally cannot be a trustworthy counterparty externally, because its stated position isn't actually held by its members — so its representative's commitment doesn't bind, and the other group is right to discount it."

**Ruled 2026-09-09 (founder): this is a BELT EXTENSION, not a core hit.** The apparent tension with the hard core's "the unit of intervention is a **human relationship**" ([research-programme.md](../../docs/research-programme.md)) dissolves on inspection: the mechanism runs *through* internal dyadic verification rather than around it, so at no point is verified understanding achieved without a counterparty holding first-person rejection authority — which is what the core-hit signature actually tests. The hard core stands unchanged; groups-as-units is a **composition rule** over relationships, not a new unit.

**What the draft must therefore not say:** that a group verifies. A group's trustworthiness as a counterparty is an *emergent property of the dyadic verification inside it* — the group never acquires the authority to say "that's not what I meant." Writing it the other way round would smuggle in the core hit the ruling just rejected.

## The mechanism worth keeping

**Unverified agreement is the socially attractive default because it preserves an exit.** This is a *strategic* account of resistance to verification, distinct from the *face-cost* account the project already runs on (Pinker, a24 / a10 / a29). Do not present it as new if the draft ends up restating deniability — check a29 §"non-disclosure norm is a rational hedge" first and either sharpen the difference or drop this section.
