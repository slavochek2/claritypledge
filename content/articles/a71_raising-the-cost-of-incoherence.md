# It Doesn't Stop Bad Actors

> Working title — `[FOUNDER DECISION: title]`. Alternatives: "The Modest Claim That Survived" · "What a Timestamped Position Actually Costs You Later."

## Arc

**ARC-1 (Tested and Tightened)** — the conversation opened on a large claim (shared clarity as a corrective mechanism for capitalism's incoherence) and spent the session cutting it down. What survived is small, defensible, and more interesting than what it replaced.

## The sentence the founder flagged

Marked `[/cpseems like usp? or theory of change? of what? vision?::]` — the founder's own uncertainty about where it belongs is part of the story:

> "it doesn't stop bad actors, it raises the cost of sustained incoherence and removes plausible deniability over time"

**Routing recommendation from the 2026-09-09 sync run: this is a theory-of-change claim, not a USP.** It describes how the world changes if the thing spreads; it promises the buyer nothing. Selling it as a USP would be selling a civilizational benefit to someone with a Tuesday problem.

## Why the retreat is the article

The strong version — clarity as a counterweight to capitalism — cannot be defended and does not need to be. The bounded version makes a **mechanical** claim: a timestamped, publicly held position is an asset that can be checked against a later position. That does not stop anyone from being incoherent. It makes sustained incoherence progressively more expensive, and it removes the "I never said that" move.

This is the same costly-signal shape as a63, a65 and a66: give up the big claim in public, keep the sharper one.

## The hole, and it must stay visible

**How does adoption spread from communities that opted in to the actors whose incoherence is profitable?** Unanswered in the source conversation and unanswered in the repo. The mechanism works *inside* a group that already accepts it; the theory of change requires it to reach people who have every reason to refuse. An article that hides this is doing the thing the product is against.

Adjacent, from a different conversation and worth one line rather than a section: crypto/DAO culture "assumes mechanism replaces comprehension, while ClarityPledge argues mechanism cannot skip comprehension" (2026-08-26) — a concrete instance of a population that would refuse.
