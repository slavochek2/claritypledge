# Thirteen of Thirteen

> Working title — `[FOUNDER DECISION: title]`. Alternatives: "Unanimity Is Not Data" · "Nobody Assigned the Roles."

## Arc

**ARC-4 (The Assumption Nobody Questioned)** — the accepted premise is that an opt-in collected in the room measures commitment to the principle. The article shows the hidden dependency (the room itself), traces what it does to a prediction that was registered before the event, and relocates the measurement.

## Idea

The first Clarity Night, the first event in this series. Every attendee was offered the meeting principle with an explicit opt-out. All of them opted in. Three joined the follow-on group afterwards, and three more wanted to but lived elsewhere.

The unanimous opt-in felt like the headline. It is the least informative number the evening produced. An opt-in taken in a room, with the host present and everyone around you saying yes, partly measures the room. A number that every attendee gives cannot separate anyone from anyone.

**Why it matters:** the opt-in is meant to be a real choice. A yes given by someone who heard the idea minutes earlier, in a room where everyone else says yes, is not yet an informed choice.

**The relocation:** move the explanation and the choice into registration. People watch a short explanation of what cognitive understanding is and choose alone before they arrive. In the room they can still change their answer, and the host invites them to. Expect the rate to drop, and read the drop as more honest answers.

## The second finding: nobody assigned the roles

The protocol needs one person speaking and one listening. Left alone for ten minutes, strangers did not assign roles. They made small talk, asked "what's your position," and never ran the exercise. The proposed fix, untested until the next event, is not a better explanation but an object: a two-sided lanyard card, Speaker on one side and Listener on the other, flipped in about a second. The bet is that a rule plus a visible token beats a rule alone. The founder's instinct to build a digital version (microphone, recording, pairing) got cut back to a card that costs a few cents (ordered, not yet used), because pairing and battery friction was exactly the setup overhead attendees had just asked to remove.

## Why article-worthy

Every workshop and community builder counts show-of-hands buy-in. The general lesson is that **a unanimous in-room commitment is a measurement of social conformity until shown otherwise**, and it can silently disable a pre-registered test while reading as validation. The role-assignment finding generalises too: a behavioural protocol that depends on people spontaneously adopting roles will not run among strangers without a physical prompt.
