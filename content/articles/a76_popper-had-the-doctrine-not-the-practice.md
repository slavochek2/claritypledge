# Popper Had the Doctrine, Not the Practice

> Working title, `[FOUNDER DECISION: title]`. Alternatives: "Fallibilism Spreads by Practice, Not Argument" · "The Compartments Shrink."

## Arc

**ARC-1 (Tested and Tightened).** The conversation opened on a large claim (verified comprehension is how people with different epistemologies make progress, and maybe the antidote to the sense-making crisis) and spent the session cutting it down under five objections. What survived is a smaller, testable conjecture plus a story the author tells against his own hero.

## Idea

**The opening story:** Popper gave us fallibilism, knowledge grows by hunting our own errors. By many accounts the same Popper misread Hegel and Plato, handled criticism of his own work badly, and fell out with close allies (Bartley among them). In the founder's terms he would have failed the Ideological Turing Test on Hegel. **The doctrine was right; the practice did not follow.** Knowing you might be wrong is not the same as checking, with the other person, whether you understood them.

**The chain** (the founder's, from the source conversation): values are revealed by what you sacrifice for → having values means measuring how consistently you follow them, which is integrity → checking your consistency only for confirmation is performing, not checking, so integrity forces fallibilism (intellectual integrity) → and how do you know you understood the rival epistemologies you reject? You verify, which is intellectual honesty: knowing when you don't understand.

**Objections and how they fared** (worth keeping in the piece, that is the ARC-1 shape):
1. *"The protocol assumes fallibilism."* Handled: anyone discovers it empirically in one restatement.
2. *Illusion of asymmetric insight (Pronin et al. 2001): people blame the other for misunderstanding.* Handled by tracking your own listening calibration, predicted score vs. given score, over many conversations.
3. *Rejecting the scorer.* Mostly handled: a judge against a fixed master copy; rejecting every scorer is visibly opting out.
4. *Festinger, When Prophecy Fails: falsification makes people double down.* Partly handled: no rival model was on offer, and the members who waited alone mostly gave up the belief. Alternative + social support may be what decides.
5. *People who understand a rival still don't shift (Plantinga).* Reframed, not settled, into the conjecture below.
6. *The consistent extremist / the self-auditing monk.* Integrity → intellectual integrity holds, but checking can stay inside the value system. Only the last step (verified understanding of rivals) does the moral work, and nothing logically forces it. It takes encounters, belonging, practice.

**What survived, as a conjecture (UNTESTED):** *recursively verified understanding of rival epistemologies shrinks dogmatic compartments.* Test: score stance in several domains before and after someone verifiably restates a rival epistemology at a high score, against matched controls.

**The landing:** dogma spreads through belonging and ritual, philosophy through argument (and mostly loses), science through results and institutions. A comprehension norm spreads through practice, so it has to compete on belonging too. That is why a practice community (hikes included) is not a side activity. Say openly that the protocol carries fallibilism in by practice, or a critic will call it a Trojan horse.

**Enrichment from 2026-10-24 ("Chinese explanation of communication"):** a worked example of honest but not intellectually honest. Li asks Wang to keep a health problem "between us"; Wang sincerely agrees and tells his wife, because to him "between us" includes her. Li feels betrayed. Wang never lied. Li tested the wrong thing: "is he honest?" instead of "does he know when he doesn't understand me?" Fictional names, keep them fictional.

## Why article-worthy

It turns the argument on its own founding figure, which is the move a Popperian audience respects, and it ends in a falsifiable prediction rather than a manifesto. It also gives the cleanest statement yet of why a practice community exists.
