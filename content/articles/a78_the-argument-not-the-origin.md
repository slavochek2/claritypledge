# The Argument, Not the Origin

> Working title, `[FOUNDER DECISION: title]`. Alternative: "Why Communication? I Can't Tell You When, Only Why."

## Arc

**ARC-1 (Tested and Tightened).** The founder answered "how did you get interested in communication?" with a derivation. Pushback in the source chat: a derivation is a reconstruction, not an origin story, and readers notice if it pretends to be one. The version that survives names that gap openly.

## Idea

**The question people ask:** "Why are you interested in communication?" The honest answer: everything is communication, and there's no single moment to point to.

**The derivation offered instead:**
1. People who want to predict or change behaviour study how humans behave.
2. A large class of unsolved problems needs cooperation or coordination, so it needs communication.
3. So maybe the breakthrough has to come in communication itself: its technologies, or how people are taught to do it.
4. Communication = sending + receiving. Where is the problem? In receiving.

**The step that must be argued, not asserted:** step 4 is the thesis and the point a skeptic will push on. It needs two or three sentences, e.g. the sender cannot verify their own success; only the receiver's reconstruction shows what arrived.

**Honest framing:** "I can't point to a moment. What I can give you is the argument that keeps me here." That separates what is known from what would be made up, which is the protocol applied to oneself.
