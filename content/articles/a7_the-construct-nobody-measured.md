# The Construct Nobody Measured

## The idea

Conversational comprehension calibration — the listener's accuracy at knowing whether they understood the speaker — has no published measurement in the literature.

Speaker-side overconfidence is documented: speakers think they're understood when they're not (Keysar & Henly 2002: believed understood 72%, actual 61%). Closeness doesn't help (Savitsky et al. 2011: spouses no more accurate than strangers). Internal states are 2x less visible than assumed (Gilovich et al. 1998).

Reader-side calibration is documented: people's self-assessed reading comprehension correlates r=0.178 with actual comprehension — barely better than chance (Yang et al. 2023, N=15,889).

But the listener's side in conversation — "I think I understood you" vs speaker-verified "you actually didn't" — has zero published measurement. The construct doesn't exist yet. /live is the first instrument that measures it.

## Angle

Not "here's why communication fails" (everyone writes that). Instead: "here's a specific thing science has never measured, and why that gap matters."

Position ClarityPledge as filling a gap in the literature, not just productizing existing research.

## Potential structure

- Hook: there's a number for how bad you are at knowing what you read (r=0.178). There's no number for how bad you are at knowing what someone told you.
- Speaker-side evidence (Keysar, Gilovich, Savitsky, Collins) — all documented, all about speakers being wrong
- Reader-side evidence (Yang et al.) — documented, but it's reading, not conversation
- The gap: nobody measured the listener in conversation. Why?
- What /live does: listener rates confidence → explains back → speaker verifies. First measurement of conversational comprehension calibration.
- Implications: if reading is r=0.178 and conversation adds tone, ego, time pressure — it's probably worse.
