## Thesis (deductively argued)

1. Coordination requires common knowledge (Pinker, Lewis, Aumann)
2. Common knowledge requires verification mechanisms (Chwe — rituals, direct speech)
3. Default conversation lacks verification (paraphrasing is socially costly — humiliation risk)
4. Without verification, participants exit with divergent private beliefs
5. False beliefs persist once formed (belief perseverance literature)
6. False beliefs propagate faster than verified knowledge (no friction vs. costly verification)
7. **Conclusion:** Structural rate asymmetry → coordination failures compound at every scale

## The unmeasured construct

Listener-side conversational comprehension calibration — "I think I understood you" vs speaker-verified "you actually didn't" — has zero published measurement.

- Speaker-side overconfidence documented: believed understood 72%, actual 61% (Keysar & Henly 2002)
- Closeness doesn't help: spouses no more accurate than strangers (Savitsky et al. 2011)
- Internal states 2x less visible than assumed (Gilovich et al. 1998)
- Reader-side: self-assessed reading comprehension r=0.178 with actual (Yang et al. 2023, N=15,889)
- Listener-side in conversation: **zero published measurement**

## Novelty claim

Nobody has framed this as a **rate asymmetry** between two measurable quantities, or connected it to a **missing verification protocol** at the interpersonal level as the root bottleneck. Sits in unclaimed intersection of Pinker, Chwe, and misinformation literature.

## Prior art (close but not the same claim)

- **Pinker** (2025, *When Everyone Knows That Everyone Knows*) — structure of common knowledge, not throughput
- **Chwe** (*Rational Ritual*, 2001) — how CK is generated, not the deficit
- **McGinnis** (Law & Liberty review) — distinguishes common belief from common knowledge, closest to our claim
- **De Freitas, Thomas, DeScioli & Pinker (2019, PNAS)** — pluralistic ignorance as survival mechanism for false norms
- **Vosoughi et al.** — false news propagates 6x faster (quantitative evidence for rate asymmetry)
