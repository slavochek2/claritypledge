## Story

I love these coworking meetups. I'm grateful for the organizer — she's put real effort into building this community. But something shifted. The café started adding rules: buy a drink, then two drinks plus food, then wear a numbered badge so they can track your spending. A new guy showed up last time and said the number thing was ridiculous. I've stopped going, and it bothers me because I don't want to stop. I think there's a blindspot here. The café owner doesn't understand user psychology — that surveillance and pressure kills the feeling of freedom. And the organizer, who I'd expect to get this, seems to accept it because "it needs to be profitable." I don't even know if she's making money from this arrangement. If she is, that should be transparent. I'd rather not organize my own meetups. I'd prefer hers to just work. But it doesn't feel right anymore, and I have a need for this kind of community — so I'll probably end up doing it myself.

## Points

1. "The person who profits from an arrangement often can't see what makes it intolerable to others — the cost is invisible to whoever doesn't pay it." — You: +3

2. "Visible tracking changes behavior even when people would have done the 'right' thing anyway — the measurement itself creates resentment." — You: +3

3. "When a space stops feeling free, people leave before they can explain why — the rule-maker sees 'unreasonable' departure, not 'reasonable' response to surveillance." — You: +3
