# Story Arcs

Reusable narrative patterns for conversation-sourced content.
Updated by `/claude-conversations-to-cp` and `/prepare-blog`.

Each arc is an abstract storytelling shape — HOW you tell the story, not WHAT it's about.
The same conversation can fit multiple arcs depending on the article angle.

---

### ARC-1: Tested and Tightened

**Pattern:** Claim stated boldly → each link tested under pressure → weak links exposed and patched → tightened version offered with the vulnerability named.

**What makes it compelling:** The reader watches rigor happen in real time. Naming the weakest link builds trust — you're not selling, you're stress-testing.

**Raw material needed:** A falsifiable claim chain, systematic objections, clear verdict per link.

**Example conversations:**
- Verification as prerequisite for correcting false beliefs (Mar 26) — 6-link chain tested, Link 4 weakest
- Understanding through mutual reflection (Mar 27) — 5 claims extracted, falsified, verdicts given

### ARC-2: The Invisible Made Visible

**Pattern:** Something important that nobody measures → why it went unmeasured (cost, social friction, wrong tools) → instrument built → first measurement → what it reveals.

**What makes it compelling:** The reader discovers a blindspot they didn't know they had. The instrument makes the abstract concrete.

**Raw material needed:** A measurement gap with evidence it exists, a working tool, real data from using it.

**Example conversations:**
- Operationalizing false belief detection (Mar 27) — truth scores as measurement mechanism
- Understanding through mutual reflection (Mar 27) — "no published study measures listener calibration in conversation"

### ARC-3: The Stranger Proved It

**Pattern:** Abstract framework exists → unexpected real-world encounter validates it → pattern recognized across cases → implications drawn.

**What makes it compelling:** Theory meets life. The stranger doesn't know the framework — their behavior IS the evidence. Anecdote carries theory.

**Raw material needed:** A real encounter or session, a framework it validates, ideally multiple instances showing the same pattern.

**Example conversations:**
- Hostel resident clarity pledge verification (Mar 26) — stranger completed protocol, named concrete past cost, wants to share

### ARC-4: The Assumption Nobody Questioned

**Pattern:** Widely accepted premise identified → show it has a hidden dependency or gap → trace consequences of the gap → propose the fix.

**What makes it compelling:** Intellectual judo — uses the reader's existing knowledge to reveal something they missed. The "oh, Popper never thought about THAT" moment.

**Raw material needed:** A well-known framework or thinker, a precise gap you can name, a connection to your instrument/protocol.

**Example conversations:**
- Understanding through mutual reflection (Mar 27) — Popper's falsification assumes language is transparent; Habermas presupposes comprehension
- Finding independent applied epistemologists (Mar 27) — uniqueness audit showing the gap in the field

### ARC-5: The Encounter That Broke It

**Pattern:** Framework carried into the wild → unplanned encounter → the encounter does NOT validate it → a silent assumption is exposed and named → the missing layer is specified.

**What makes it compelling:** The inverse of ARC-3. ARC-3's stranger *proves* the theory; ARC-5's stranger *breaks* it, and the author reports the break rather than the win. Costly-signal credibility: an author who publishes his own falsifications is doing the thing the product sells. Requires the author to be the one who was wrong.

**Distinct from ARC-1:** ARC-1 tests a claim under argumentative pressure (a conversation). ARC-5 tests it against reality (an encounter or a field deployment), and the failure is behavioral, not logical.

**Raw material needed:** A real encounter or field deployment, a specific assumption it falsified, an honest account of the author's own move that failed, and the layer the break reveals is missing.

**Example conversations:**
- Understanding across cultural and personal boundaries (Jul 16) — protocol run unannounced on a stranger; she disengaged; exposed the missing consent layer and the score-implies-articulable-delta assumption
- Сильн (Jul 14) — field practitioner dropped the numeric scoring and the effect persisted; exposed scoring as possibly not load-bearing

### ARC-6: The Refutation That Never Ran

**Pattern:** A position is abandoned under argumentative pressure → each concession is a valid-sounding answer to an objection → the concessions form a monotone direction (usually widening scope) → the pattern is named → the missing element turns out to be a *test*, not a better argument → a pre-commitment guard is installed.

**What makes it compelling:** The inverse of ARC-1. ARC-1's claim is tested and *earns* its retreat; ARC-6's claim was never tested and retreats anyway. The author is the one who was wrong, and the reader can catch the error mid-flight rather than after it's been tidied up. Costly-signal credibility with a built-in lesson: "this might fail" is not evidence that it did.

**Distinct from ARC-5:** ARC-5's break comes from reality (an encounter, a field deployment) and is behavioral. ARC-6's break never happens — the failure is that a *conjecture* was accepted as a refutation, and the story is about the missing test.

**Raw material needed:** A traceable sequence of position changes, the objection behind each, evidence that none of them was a test, and a nameable direction the sequence moved in.

**Example conversations:**
- Internal feedback loops and cross-departmental communication (Jul 26) — buyer moved cofounder → COO → acquirer → citizen assembly in one hour, each step answering an argument, none answering a test
