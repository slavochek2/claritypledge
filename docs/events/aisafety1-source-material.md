## The news peg — why this evening, now

**Sanders / Casar, Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act, announced 3 September 2026.**
Permanently bans superintelligent AI, temporarily pauses advanced AI development pending a federal
regulator, and directs the US to pursue international agreements. Summarised as stopping *"AI
oligarchs from building machines humans cannot control."*
Sources: [press release](https://www.sanders.senate.gov/press-releases/news-sanders-casar-introduce-legislation-to-ban-artificial-superintelligence-and-temporarily-pause-advanced-ai-development/)
· [Common Dreams](https://www.commondreams.org/news/sanders-casar-ai-superintelligence)
· [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/03/bernie-sanders-superintelligence-ban-ai-pause)
· [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/bernie-sanders-calls-for-global-ban-on-ai-superintelligence-2000806829)

**UK: Labour MP Alex Sobel** introduced a bill to ban superintelligence, reported as the first such
bill in any G7 parliament. A Ten Minute Rule bill, so unlikely to progress without government
cooperation. Sources: [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/09/08/ban-superintelligence-ai-uk-us-lawmakers/)
· [House of Lords Library](https://lordslibrary.parliament.uk/superintelligent-ai-should-its-development-be-stopped/)

**Do not write "Sanders proposes a G7 ban."** His bill is US federal. The G7 framing belongs to the
UK bill. Different person, different country. This conflation was made once already and corrected.

## The Sanders finding — the strongest thing to say in the room

**Verified first-hand 2026-09-10** by opening the post in a real browser and counting.

Our agent placed Sanders on the **ownership** side of P3: the danger is who owns AI, *not* that the
machine slipped control.

His [X post of 3 September 2026](https://x.com/BernieSanders/status/2095542398084415952), 8.4M
views at time of reading, runs **4,313 characters**. Across it:

- **"control" appears 8 times**
- **"oligarch" appears once**, at character 4,148 of 4,313 — **96% of the way through**, in the
  second-to-last sentence

Closing lines, verbatim:

> "Let me be clear: A superintelligent AI that escapes human control will not be an American
> problem. It will not be a Chinese problem. It will be humanity's problem. My legislation would
> direct the federal government to not just stop superintelligence here in the United States, but
> to work to prevent it from being developed anywhere around the world. The future of humanity
> cannot be left in the hands of a handful of Big Tech oligarchs."

**Why it matters:** the instrument made a checkable claim about where a person stands, and within
six days the public record moved against it. Any reader can verify it in thirty seconds from the
same URL. That is the falsifiability demonstration, and no competitor can imitate it, because they
have no position on record to be wrong about.

**Placement, decided:** this is event material — the strongest thing to say out loud in the room.
It is **not** article material and it is **not** the title. A title built on it promises a story the
reading surface does not tell.

## Title

Working title, agreed 2026-09-10: **"Sanders, LeCun, Bengio, Leahy: Four Sentences on AI. Where Do
You Stand?"**

Article name and event name are the same by decision. Two constraints learned the hard way:

- **A comma list, never "Sanders vs LeCun, Bengio, Leahy."** Sanders shares a point with Leahy only,
  and none at all with LeCun or Bengio. The "vs" form misstates the structure.
- **Not "AI power concentration."** It describes one of four points, and it reverts to the framing
  the founder explicitly steered away from: *"I wouldn't need to focus only on monopolization. I
  mean, generally, AI safety of different kinds."*

A 40-candidate generate-and-score round produced no usable winner, because the criteria demanded a
Pinker-style noun phrase *and* a working calendar-invite line, which are close to mutually
exclusive. Do not re-run it without fixing that.
