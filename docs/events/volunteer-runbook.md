# Volunteer Runbook — Clarity Night

You are helping run a Clarity Night. This page is everything you need. It is written to be read on
a phone, standing up, five minutes before the doors open.

The host runs the evening. You handle the door, the room and the things the host cannot do while
talking to people.

---

## The evening in one paragraph

People arrive, put on a badge, and sit in threes at numbered tables. There are three rounds of
about 15 minutes. In each round, two people talk and one observes; then everyone moves to a new
table with new people. The host rings a bell to end each round. At the end everyone answers a few
questions on their phone and hears the date of the next one.

---

## Before people arrive

- Badges laid out at the entrance, where people walk in
- Table numbers visible on every table
- Chairs in threes

## At the door

- **Give everyone a badge.** Two sides: orange **S · SPEAKER**, blue **L · LISTENER**. They flip it
  depending on their role in the round. Everyone wears one.
- **First time here?** Point them at the event page on their phone and let them do the short
  onboarding standing up. Do not hold the room for them.
- **Arriving late?** Welcome them, and tell them: *"You'll join at the next round — about N minutes."*
  They do not need to catch up on anything. Nobody repeats anything for them.

## During the evening

- **Watch for people with nowhere to sit.** A table of two is fine. A person standing alone is not
  — walk them to a table with space.
- **Do not join a conversation to fix it.** Groups that drift off topic are allowed to drift. Silence
  is allowed. Resist the urge to facilitate.
- **If someone looks lost**, the answer is almost always: *"Check your phone — it shows your table
  and whether you're speaking, listening or observing."*
- **Someone asks what the numbers are about:** people say how well they think they were understood,
  0 to 10. It is a self-estimate, not a score of the other person. That is the whole thing.

## Recording — what to say if asked

Some people wear a small clip-on microphone and are recording their own conversation.

- **If someone asks whether they are being recorded:** yes, if someone at their table is wearing a
  mic. They can ask that person to pause at any time, and that person will pause. No explanation
  needed.
- **Never ask the room who does not want to be recorded.** Nobody should have to announce that in
  front of other people.
- **Never point out who did or did not want to be recorded.**
- The person wearing the mic is responsible for telling their table. Not you.

## If the technology fails

This is normal and the evening does not depend on it.

- **Phones not showing tables?** The host handles the regrouping out loud. Help people find their
  table numbers.
- **Projector dead?** The host talks instead. Nothing is lost.
- **Someone's phone is flat?** They do not need it. Tell them which table and which role.

## At the end

- Help the host get everyone to answer the closing questions on their phone before they drift off —
  this is the only data from the evening, and once people stand up it is gone.
- **The group photo.** Remind the host if they forget. Ask the room first; anyone who prefers not to
  be in it steps out of frame.
- Collect leftover badges and lanyards.
- Count the microphones back in.

---

## What not to do

- Do not explain the method. If someone wants the theory, point them at the host or the website.
- Do not tell people what to talk about.
- Do not take photos of individual people without asking them.
- Do not share anything you overhear. People say real things in these rooms, and they do so because
  they expect it to stay there.
