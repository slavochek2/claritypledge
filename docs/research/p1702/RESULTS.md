## 1. The model of record

Code, in `src/research/cheap-gap-reveal/`:
- `strategies.ts`: parameters and strategies.
- `solver.ts`: the exact solution.
- `model.ts`: the game definition, Monte Carlo and payoff matrices.
- `dynamics.ts`: selection.
- `exp-*.ts`: one module per experiment, with the verdict rules.
- `stats.ts`, `grid.ts`, `prng.ts`: helpers.

**Game.** Repeated donation game, c = 1, b ∈ {2, 3, 4} (main b = 3). Continuation probability
**δ = 0.9** (expected 10 rounds), not a fixed number of rounds. Payoffs are **per round**, meaning
(1 − δ) × expected total over the encounter. Pairwise payoffs are computed **exactly** as a finite Markov
chain over the two players' joint memory states, so they carry no sampling error. A Monte Carlo
simulator of the same game is used only to validate the exact solver: 8 pairs × 200,000 games, every
|z| < 0.9 (`mc_validation.csv`).

**Two error sources, kept apart:**
- **Slip** ε_s: the intended move is executed as its opposite. A player knows its own realized move.
  No check can prevent a slip.
- **Misreading** ε_m: before acting, the player misreads the partner's previous realized move, and acts
  and updates its memory as if that move were the opposite. This is the "error in understanding" of
  Boerlijst, Nowak & Sigmund 1997, who already use that term for misperceiving the other's previous
  move. There is no misreading in round 1. The spec's separate "perception" and "understanding" sources
  are collapsed into this one; see Limitations.

**Checks.** Available only in a share m ∈ {0.2, 0.5, 1} of encounters ("checkable"). **Consent rule,
used everywhere:** a checking strategy checks only if the encounter is checkable *and* the partner is
willing. Willing means the partner is itself a checker, or displays the badge (FAKER, BADGE). Each
checker pays its own cost and fixes only its own misreadings. The cost is charged on **every move** of
such an encounter, round 1 included, which is conservative against the checker.

- **PARA** (always-paraphrase): pays r per move, and a misreading is removed with probability q.
- **GAP** (gap-reveal-then-bridge): pays d per move. A detector flags an actual misreading with
  probability s and a correct reading with probability a (a false alarm). On a flag it pays r, and a
  flagged misreading is fixed with probability q. Expected cost per move:
  κ = d + r·(ε_m·s + (1 − ε_m)·a).
- **CTRL** (equivalence control): pays exactly κ per move and gets the same expected cut in its own
  error events **in every round**, untargeted. The matching argument:
  - GAP can repair only a misreading, and a misreading exists only from round 2 on, since it is a
    misreading of the previous move. So GAP cuts nothing in round 1 and ε_m·s·q in every later round.
  - CTRL slips at the plain rate ε_s in round 1. From round 2 on, both its slip and misreading rates
    are cut by the factor k = ε_m·s·q / (ε_s + ε_m).
  - Expected own error events per round, round 1: ε_s for both.
  - Round t ≥ 2: GAP has ε_s + ε_m(1 − s·q); CTRL has (ε_s + ε_m)(1 − k) = ε_s + ε_m − ε_m·s·q, the same.
  - The match holds in every round, so it holds under any discounting.
  - Before the review fixes, CTRL also cut its slips in round 1 (§7, change 7).
- **CTRL_M** (second equivalence control, added in review): pays κ per move and cuts the misreading rate by
  the same ε_m·s·q, untargeted, leaving slips alone. Misreadings here are independent events with one
  rate, so a cut "targeted at detected misreadings" and an untargeted cut of the same size give the same
  rate. **CTRL_M is GAP by construction** (mis = ε_m − ε_m·s·q = ε_m(1 − s·q)). Its ties with GAP are a
  definitional identity, not evidence. All it shows is that in this model GAP is exactly "pay for fewer
  misreadings", nothing more. It cannot show that understanding does not matter, because this model
  has no way for understanding to do anything a lower misreading rate does not.

All three checkers use **TFT as their base rule**: verification instead of forgiveness, as the
conjecture states. Outside a check they are plain TFT.

**Other strategies:** ALLC, ALLD, TFT; GTFT with the Nowak–Sigmund generosity
g = min(1 − (T−R)/(R−S), (R−P)/(T−P)) = 1 − c/b; TF2T; contrite TFT (CTFT, standing rule quoted from
Boerlijst et al.); WSLS; and Prudent Pavlov (PPAV) in a memory-2 form: cooperate iff the last round was
mutual C or the last two rounds were mutual D. That form follows Boerlijst et al.'s description ("after
any defection it will only resume cooperation after two rounds of mutual defection"). It is an
approximation of their standing-based version.
- **APOLOGY:** TFT that, after its own slip, pays γ = 1 to the partner, and treats an apologised
  defection as cooperation. **Assumption:** an accepted apology overrides a misreading, so an
  apologised D is always read as C. The apology channel itself is noiseless, while a check only
  reduces misreadings by s·q. This favours APOLOGY. APOLOGY never won a single cell of the main grid
  (§3), so the assumption cannot have produced any verdict above.
- **FAKE_APOL:** always defects and apologises every time, exploiting APOLOGY.
- **FAKER:** shows the badge, never pays for a check, and always intends to defect. Like every player it
  slips, so it cooperates by accident with probability ε_s (as do ALLD and FAKE_APOL). Checkers pay to
  check it.
- **BADGE:** shows the badge, plays TFT, never checks. Used in experiment 5 only.

**Parameters.**

| Parameter | Values | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ε_s, ε_m | {0, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1} | all 15 non-zero combinations |
| d | {0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.25} | |
| r | {0.5, 1, 2} | |
| s, a | 0.9, 0.1 | |
| q | {0.45, 0.9, 1.0} | 0.9 ± 50%; +50% (1.35) is capped at 1 |
| γ | 1 | fixed before the first run |

Invalid inputs throw instead of returning NaN:
- δ outside [0, 1), a probability outside [0, 1], or a negative cost;
- a game that is not a donation dilemma (b > c > 0 required);
- a checkable share m outside [0, 1], an unknown strategy id, or two matrix sets with different ids;
- a singular solve, or a reducible chain in the log-space GTH;
- N < 2 or a non-finite β in the stationary distribution;
- an impossible regular graph;
- an ABM with N ≤ 1, burn ≥ MCS, a non-finite β, or an out-of-range strategy index;
- a NaN reaching any tie-aware comparison. A missing value never counts as a win or a loss.

**Selection.** Well-mixed population, N = 100, pairwise-comparison (Fermi) imitation with
β ∈ {0.1, 1, 10}. For the strategy competitions (experiments 2 to 4): the low-mutation limit
(Fudenberg & Imhof). Fixation probabilities are exact (closed-form product formula, computed in log
space), and the stationary distribution of the embedded chain comes from a log-space GTH elimination, so
it stays accurate at β = 10, where fixation probabilities fall to about 10⁻³⁰⁰⁰. This is valid because
there are no committed players in these experiments.

**Numerical ties.** Two abundances that agree to within 10⁻⁹ of the larger one count as a tie. The
same applies to a slope within 10⁻⁹ of its series' abundance scale. A tie is neither a win nor a loss.
- Why relative: abundances span hundreds of orders of magnitude, so an absolute tolerance either misses
  float noise on large values or swallows real differences between rare strategies.
- Solver noise is about 10⁻¹⁵ relative.
- Sensitivity of the equivalence counts to this choice is reported in §4.
- "Most abundant" means strictly above second place. Ranks are mid-ranks: tied strategies share the
  mean of their ranks.

**Pools:**
- BASE = {ALLC, ALLD, TFT, GTFT, TF2T, CTFT, WSLS, PPAV, PARA, GAP}.
- FULL = BASE + {APOLOGY, FAKE_APOL, FAKER}.
- FULL_CTRL = FULL + CTRL.
- FULL_SWAP = FULL with CTRL in place of GAP.
- FULL_NOPP = FULL without PPAV (post-hoc).

The main grid is 1,620 cells (b, ε_s, ε_m, d, r, m) × 3 q × 3 β × 5 pools.

**Committed players (experiment 5).** Full agent-based Fermi dynamics, not the embedded chain.
- Committed agents never update, but are visible and **can be imitated** (Cardillo & Masuda rule).
- Normal agents start as ALLD ("a room of defectors"), imitate a random partner (a random neighbour on
  a graph) with probability 1/(1+e^{−β(π_j−π_i)}), and explore the FULL pool with μ = 0.001.
- Payoffs are the exact pairwise expectations.
- Cooperation is the time-averaged cooperation rate of **normal agents' moves**.

## 2. Calibration

**A. Noise anchors.** Pre-registered qualitative checks, all at b = 3, δ = 0.9 (`calibration_selfplay.csv`).

| Check | Values (self-play payoff, b − c = 2 is the maximum) | Pass |
|---|---|---|

| CTFT is robust to slips but not to misreadings (Boerlijst et al.) | CTFT 1.83 under slips vs 1.53 under misreadings (= TFT's 1.53 exactly) | yes |
| PPAV beats CTFT under misreadings | 1.64 vs 1.53 | yes |
| CTFT invades ALLD as well as TFT does, and much better than WSLS and GTFT ("PAVLOV and GTFT fare both very poorly") | log₁₀ρ: CTFT −1.38, TFT −1.41, PPAV −3.97, WSLS −8.35, GTFT −19.1 | yes |

**B. He, Shen, Shi & Tanimoto 2024 committed-TFT critical mass** (`calibration_he2024.csv`).
- Setting: weak PD with R = 1, T = 1.1, S = P = 0, long-run payoffs, no noise. Well-mixed, N = 1000,
  normals C or D starting 50/50, 5 seeds.
- **With non-imitable zealots** (normals stay C or D): full cooperation is reached at ρ ≈ 0.085 to 0.09
  (β = 10: FC = 0.87 at ρ = 0.08, 1.0 at 0.085; β = 100: 0.92 at 0.085, 1.0 at 0.09). This matches He
  et al.'s reported ρ ≈ 0.09, and the mean-field condition ρ > (T−R)/T = 0.0909.
- **Not reproduced:** He et al. report extinction of cooperation (FC = 0) below the threshold. This
  simulator gives a stable interior mixture that tracks the mean-field FC = 10ρ/(1−ρ) within 0.01
  (for example FC = 0.41 at ρ = 0.04). Their main update rule (replicator-like, degree-normalised) was
  not implemented, so the cause is unverified.
- **With imitable zealots** (the rule used in experiment 5): FC = 1 already at ρ = 0.02, the smallest
  non-zero value tested. Normals copy committed TFT. **The zealot rule alone moves the critical mass by
  more than 4×.** Any f* below inherits this dependence.
- Correction recorded: run 1 measured He et al.'s FC as the share of pure-C normals. That under-counts
  cooperation when normals imitate committed TFT, and gave FC = 0 at every ρ. Fixed to the cooperation
  rate of normals' moves, and the non-imitable variant was added. Neither change touches a conjecture
  parameter.

**Controls on the pipeline itself.** These are post-hoc, added because every GAP verdict came back
negative (`control_probes.csv`). They test whether the pipeline can rank GAP first at all. All use a
free, perfect check (d = r = 0, s = q = 1, a = 0), FULL pool, β = 1, m = 1, unless stated.

| Probe | Result |
|---|---|
| Misreading-only noise (ε_m = 0.1) | GAP and PARA tie at the top, 0.500 each (they are the same strategy when free); PPAV 1.7·10⁻⁷ |
| Same, vs {ALLC, ALLD, TFT, GTFT} only | GAP 1.000 |
| Noise split ε_s = ε_m = 0.05 | PPAV 0.978, GAP 0.0005 |
| Slip sweep at ε_m = 0.1: ε_s = 10⁻⁸, 10⁻⁶, 10⁻⁴, 10⁻³, 0.01 | GAP and PARA tie at the top in every case (GAP 0.49999989 down to 0.49993) |
| Slip sweep at ε_m = 0.1: ε_s = 0.05 | ALLD 0.27 on top; GAP and PARA 0.157 each |
| Default costs, misreading-only (ε_m = 0.05), pool {ALLC, ALLD, TFT, GAP} | ALLD 0.82, GAP 0.084, TFT 0.050 |

The known-good cases come out with GAP first, so the pipeline can rank GAP on top and the refutation is
informative. **Limitation of the control:** in the main known-good probe, GAP only *ties* PARA. Under
Criterion 2's own strict rule ("single most abundant"), that tie would not count as a win. The probe
that does give a strict GAP win is the reduced pool {ALLC, ALLD, TFT, GTFT} (GAP 1.000). So the only
strict-win control runs without the other checker and without exploiters.

**What the probes show about slips, and no more.** A free, perfect check stays at the top (tied with
PARA) with ε_m = 0.1 for every slip rate up to ε_s = 0.01. It loses at ε_s = 0.05, both with
ε_m = 0.1 and with ε_m = 0.05. An earlier version of this file said a TFT-based checker loses "as soon
as there are any slips" even when free. That claim was wrong: a reviewer's counterexample at
ε_s = 10⁻⁸ gives GAP ≈ PARA ≈ 0.5, and the slip sweep was added to replace the claim (§7).

## 3. Criterion 2: does the check survive? REFUTED in this restricted model

Data:
- `main_map.csv`: stationary abundances, BASE and FULL pools. It is 4.7 MB, so it is not committed.
  The command at the end of this file regenerates it byte for byte.
- `criterion2_by_condition.csv`.
- `criterion2_robust_cells.csv`: empty.

- 1,620 cells in all. 765 of them have ε_m > 0 and meet the floor κ ≥ (ε_s + ε_m)·b.
- **Robust wins (FULL pool, all of β ∈ {0.1, 10} × q ∈ {0.45, 0.9, 1.0}): 0.** The same rule in BASE
  (no exploiters) also gives 0. Floor cells where GAP is the most abundant under *any* single β and q: 0.
- Who wins instead (FULL, β = 1, q = 0.9, all 1,620 cells): PPAV 1,080, FAKER 378, CTFT 108, ALLD 54.
  GAP's median mid-rank is 11 of 13.
- Ties at the top occur only at β = 10: 1,362 FULL rows where ALLD and FAKER tie. They were counted as
  ALLD or FAKER wins before the tie rule. No verdict number changes.
- Mean GAP abundance across cells with misreading: 0.045 vs GTFT 0.047 at β = 0.1; 0.0006 vs 0.0021
  at β = 1; about 10⁻¹¹ vs 10⁻¹⁰ at β = 10 (FULL, q = 0.9).
- **GAP is strictly the most abundant strategy in only 11 rows of the grid, in 10 cells.**
  - All are at β = 0.1 and m = 1, with ε_m = 0.1, ε_s ≤ 0.01 and b ∈ {3, 4}.
  - 10 of the 11 rows have q = 1.
  - All fall **below** the cost floor (κ = 0.14 to 0.24 against ε·b = 0.30 to 0.44). That is exactly
    the "win only when cheap" case the criterion pre-registers as a refutation.
  - An earlier version said "10 cells, 9 of them with q = 1", which mixed cells and rows.
- **Post-hoc robustness:** removing PPAV (which I implemented as an approximation) does not rescue GAP.
  Without it, FULL still gives 0 robust wins and 0 floor cells where GAP is top under any β (β = 1
  included; rechecked after §7 item 30) and q. The
  winners become FAKER (662), WSLS (593), CTFT (271), ALLD (56) and TF2T (38).

Why, as a reading of the mechanics rather than a tested claim: the check removes only misreadings, while
its TFT base still echoes every slip. Where noise is pure misreading, the check is too expensive relative
to the gain at any cost above the floor. FAKER-dominated cells are those where defection wins anyway.
FAKER beats plain ALLD there because a checker trying to invade must pay to check a badge-holder.

## 4. Equivalence test: NO CONSISTENT DIFFERENCE

Data: `equivalence.csv` (column `sign_tol` is the tie-aware sign). Pool FULL_CTRL (GAP and CTRL both
present), q = 0.9, every cell with ε_m > 0 and every β: 3,888 comparisons.

- CTRL is more abundant than GAP in **58.3%** of comparisons, GAP in **16.7%**, and **25.0%** are
  numerical ties. No side reaches the pre-registered 75%.
- **The sign is set by the noise mix alone.** It is identical across every b, d, r, m and β:

| ε_s \ ε_m | 0.01 | 0.05 | 0.1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | tie | tie | tie |
| 0.01 | CTRL | **GAP** | **GAP** |
| 0.05 | CTRL | CTRL | CTRL |
| 0.1 | CTRL | CTRL | CTRL |

  - With ε_s = 0, GAP and CTRL are mechanically the same strategy, so these are ties by
    construction. Before the tie rule they were split 239 / 239 by float noise.
  - GAP leads only where misreadings outnumber slips by 5× or more.
- **The effect is tiny everywhere.** The median relative difference (x_GAP − x_CTRL)/(x_GAP + x_CTRL)
  is −0.08%, and the largest is about 7%. The 95% range of the absolute difference is
  [−0.00036, +0.00010].
- Swapping CTRL in for GAP in FULL (FULL_SWAP vs FULL) gives the same split: GAP ahead 16.7%, ties 25.0%.
- Post-hoc head-to-head, log₁₀(ρ_{GAP invades CTRL} / ρ_{CTRL invades GAP}):
  - median −0.001, with GAP favoured in 16.7% of comparisons and ties in 25.0%;
  - by β, median −0.0003 at β = 0.1, −0.003 at β = 1 and −0.03 at β = 10.
- **Sensitivity to the tie rule** (`tieRuleSensitivity` in `summary.json`):
  - no tolerance: GAP 22.8%, CTRL 64.5%;
  - an absolute |diff| < 10⁻¹² rule: GAP 11.1%, CTRL 39.1%, ties 49.8%. This rule swallows real
    differences between rare strategies at β = 10.
  - No rule puts either side at 75%.

**Second control, misreading-only (CTRL_M, pool FULL_CTRLM).** Same cells and rule. GAP and CTRL_M tie
in all 3,888 comparisons; the largest relative difference is 1.4·10⁻¹⁴, which is rounding. This is the
expected result, since CTRL_M is GAP by construction (§1): **a definitional identity, not evidence.**
It shows only that GAP is exactly "pay for fewer misreadings" in this model. So the CTRL comparison above tests only one
thing: whether the same-sized cut is better spent on misreadings or spread over slips and misreadings.
It does not test understanding against a mere lower error rate, because in this model the two cannot be
told apart.

**What changed the verdict.** Before the review fixes the verdict was CONTROL BEATS GAP: control ahead
in 81.1%, GAP ahead in 6.1%. Two fixes were applied, and each was checked separately:
- **Tie rule alone**, with the old CTRL: GAP 0%, CTRL 75.0%, ties 25.0%. The verdict stays at CONTROL
  BEATS GAP, exactly at the boundary.
- **Adding the round-1 matching of CTRL** (§1): the verdict drops to NO CONSISTENT DIFFERENCE. The old
  CTRL had an unmatched advantage, an error cut in round 1 where GAP can repair nothing.

**Plainly:** in this model, targeting the check at misreadings is neither clearly better nor clearly
worse than an untargeted error cut of the same size and cost. It is very slightly better when
misreadings strongly dominate, and very slightly worse otherwise. The differences are too small to
matter.
- The earlier claim that "the understanding framing adds nothing; the control is slightly better" was
  partly an artefact of the unmatched control, and is withdrawn.
- A candidate mechanism for the CTRL-favoured cells is untested. For a TFT base, misreading a D as a C
  is an accidental act of forgiveness, and GAP removes those along with the harmful misreadings. CTRL
  instead spreads part of its cut onto slips.

## 5. Novel prediction: NOT CORROBORATED

Data: `novel_prediction.csv`, `summary.json → novelPrediction`.
- Setup: total error ε ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.1}, misreading share σ ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1}.
- Measure: advantage = x_GAP − x_GTFT in FULL (q = 0.9), with an OLS slope on σ in each of 972 nuisance
  cells (b × d × r × m × β × ε).
- Uncertainty is the spread of slopes across nuisance cells. Payoffs are exact, so there is no sampling
  error.
- **The pre-registered rule pools all cells across β.** Everything per β below is post-hoc.

**Presence tolerance (review round).** A cell where neither GAP nor GTFT ever reaches a stationary
abundance of 10⁻¹² (at any σ) says nothing about their difference, because both are absent. Its slope
counts as a tie. This happens in 288 of 972 cells, all at β = 10, where abundances go down to 10⁻³³.
The remaining slopes are real numbers, not float noise.
- The stationary vector is solved in log space, and the abundances used for the slopes are its
  exponentials. Even the smallest of them, about 10⁻³³, is far above double-precision underflow
  (10⁻³⁰⁸), so the slopes are exact to rounding.
- The secondary log-ratio is taken directly from the log-space values (log x_GAP − log x_GTFT), so it
  cannot underflow at any β. Before round 3 it was log(x_GAP / x_GTFT) on the exponentials; on this
  grid the result is the same.

**Pooled, as pre-registered:** 40.8% of cells positive, 29.5% negative, 29.6% uninformative.
- The median cell is an uninformative one. Read literally, the rule "REFUTED if the median slope ≤ 0"
  would say REFUTED, because the median slope is 0.
- A median that lands on cells where both strategies are absent cannot refute anything, so the verdict
  is reported as **NOT CORROBORATED**. The 75% bar is not met either way.
- This reading of the rule is post-hoc and was made on reviewer advice (§7, change 17).
- How the rule is applied (§7, change 22):
  - the median is the tie-aware median *slope*, not a median of signs;
  - CORROBORATED needs ≥ 75% of cells strictly positive (ties count as not positive) and a median
    slope > 0;
  - REFUTED is a median slope < 0, or a median of 0 made only of present, flat slopes;
  - NOT CORROBORATED is a median of 0 when cells with absent strategies exist.
- Without the tolerance, as in the first run, the median slope is −1·10⁻³², inside the β = 10 cells:
  that REFUTED came from cells with no information.

**Per β (post-hoc):**

| | Median slope (per unit σ) | Positive | Negative | Uninformative | Verdict if this β alone |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| β = 0.1 | +0.0117 | 91.0% | 9.0% | 0% | would pass (CORROBORATED) |
| β = 1 | −4.9·10⁻⁵ | 20.4% | 79.6% | 0% | REFUTED |
| β = 10 | ≈ 0 | 11.1% | 0% | 88.9% | uninformative |

- β = 1 is **not** uninformative: both strategies are rare there (peak abundance 7·10⁻⁵ to 0.018), but
  present, and the slope is mostly negative.
- β = 10: **every one of the 36 cells where the strategies are present has a positive slope** (100% of
  present cells; 11.1% of all β = 10 cells). That is thin support:
  - all 36 are at ε = 0.05 and b = 2;
  - both abundances stay at or below 3.3·10⁻⁸;
  - GAP stays below GTFT at every σ, so "positive" means the gap narrows. GAP never overtakes GTFT.
- So the prediction holds under weak selection, fails under moderate selection, and cannot be assessed
  under strong selection.

**Sensitivity to the presence threshold** (pooled; `presenceSensitivity` in `summary.json`):

| Threshold | Absent cells counted as ties (pre-registered pooling) | Absent cells dropped (post-hoc) |
|---|---|---|
| none | REFUTED (45.3% positive) | same |
| 10⁻¹², 10⁻⁹ | NOT CORROBORATED (40.8%) | INCONCLUSIVE (58.0% of 684) |
| 10⁻⁶ | NOT CORROBORATED (37.1%) | INCONCLUSIVE (55.7% of 648) |
| 10⁻³ | NOT CORROBORATED (36.6%) | CORROBORATED (80.5% of 442) |

The verdict depends on how cells where the strategies are absent or rare are handled. With the
pre-registered pooling it is NOT CORROBORATED at every threshold. Only a post-hoc choice of both a 10⁻³
presence threshold *and* dropping absent cells would turn it positive, so no positive claim is made.

The log-ratio log(x_GAP/x_GTFT) (secondary, not pre-registered) rises with σ in 63.8% of cells and falls
in 6.6% (29.6% uninformative). Read honestly: shifting error toward misreadings makes the checker
relatively less bad against GTFT under weak selection. That is mechanical, since the check only acts on
misreadings. It never makes the checker abundant.

## 6. Committed minority, f*, and Criterion 3: UNINFORMATIVE by design

Data: `committed_minority.csv`, `summary.json → committedMinority`.

**Parameters**, fixed before the run: b = 3, ε_s = 0.01, ε_m = 0.05 (misreading-dominated, the
conjecture's favourable regime), d = 0.1, r = 1, q = 0.9, m = 0.5.

**Initial state and update rule:** normals start as ALLD; zealots can be imitated; μ = 0.001.

**Runs:**
- Well-mixed, N = 100: 10 seeds, 3,000 MCS (last 2,000 averaged).
- Random 4-regular graph, N = 1000: 5 seeds, 1,500 MCS (last 1,000 averaged).
- At f = 0 there are no committed players, so the three committed types share the **same** runs.
- Rule: f* = the smallest grid f where mean cooperation exceeds 0.5.
- Cooperation intervals are 95% Student-t over seeds (t₉ = 2.262, t₄ = 2.776), clamped to [0, 1].
- The uncertainty of f* itself comes from a bootstrap over seeds: 2,000 replicates; f = 0 is resampled
  jointly, since its runs are shared.

| Setting | f*(GAP: badge + check) | f*(BADGE: badge, no check) | f*(TFT: no badge) | Bootstrap 95% of f*, GAP and BADGE | Cooperation at f = 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Well-mixed, β = 1 | 0.01 | 0.01 | 0.01 | [0, 0.01] each | 0.42 [0.13, 0.70] |
| Well-mixed, β = 10 | 0.02 (0.67 [0.46, 0.89]) | 0.02 (0.63 [0.40, 0.87]) | 0.02 (0.75 [0.57, 0.93]) | [0.02, 0.03] each | 0.01 |
| Well-mixed, β = 1, ε_s = ε_m = 0.03 | 0.01 | 0.01 | 0.01 | [0, 0.01] each | 0.39 [0.14, 0.64] |
| RRG k = 4, N = 1000, β = 1 | 0 † | 0 † | 0 † | [0, 0] each | 0.81 [0.77, 0.84] |

† An f* of 0 means cooperation already exceeds 0.5 with no committed players. It is not a critical
mass. Under weak selection well-mixed, the f = 0 interval spans 0.5, so an f* of 0.01 there means only
"one committed player out of 100 lifts cooperation clearly above 0.5". It is not a sharp threshold.

**Why Criterion 3 cannot answer its question (review finding, verified on the actual matrices).** The
design is **blind against the starting population**. That covers the threshold event, not the whole
run.
- A check needs a willing partner: a checker, or a badge-holder.
- The room starts as ALLD, and 10 of the 13 FULL strategies are unwilling. Only PARA, GAP and FAKER are
  willing.
- Against every unwilling strategy, committed GAP and committed BADGE have **exactly** the same payoffs,
  in both directions (largest difference 0, `criterion3_design` in `summary.json`).
- They differ only when a GAP meets a willing partner: another committed GAP, a normal that has copied
  one, or a rare PARA or FAKER mutant (μ = 0.001).
- At the threshold, f is 0.01 to 0.03, so those encounters are a few percent of a committed player's
  encounters, and the threshold is decided almost entirely where the two types are identical.

**The design could not tell the check from the badge at the threshold, because checks need a willing
partner and the room starts with none.** The identical f* values are therefore **UNINFORMATIVE**. They are
neither evidence for nor evidence against the check. The numbers below are kept as measured.
- Once normals copy the committed type, the room does become willing, and checkers meet each other.
- The plateau difference (0.80 vs 0.74) reflects that GAP–GAP self-play. The pre-registered f* rule
  does not measure it.
- How the verdict is computed (`criterion3Verdict`): a detected f* difference is always reported as
  DIFFERS. UNINFORMATIVE applies only when nothing is detected *and* the design is blind at the start.
  The blindness is kept as a separate field, `criterion3_designLimitation`.

**Future work, as a new pre-registration (not part of this run):** a design where checks can act
against the starting room. For example:
- normals that are willing (badge-holders) from the start; or
- a check that does not need the partner's consent;

with the rule and seeds fixed before running.

**Findings:**
- **f* is found only under strong selection (β = 10, well-mixed): f* = 0.02 for all three committed
  types.** At f = 0.01, cooperation stays at 0.02 to 0.10. Under weak selection, or on the graph, there
  is no critical mass in the usual sense.
- **Criterion 3: UNINFORMATIVE (see above).** The pre-registered rule does not fire: the f* point
  estimates are identical in all four settings. The bootstrap 95% interval of f*(GAP) − f*(BADGE) is
  [0, 0] in three settings and [−0.01, 0.01] at β = 10, where 13.9% of replicates differ by one grid
  step. Given the design, this agreement was close to guaranteed.
- A committed plain TFT does as well. So in this model, "a few pledgers turn the room" holds for any
  visible committed reciprocator. Whether the check adds anything to it was not testable here.
- Given calibration B, the location of f* depends on the zealot rule by more than 4×. No number of the
  "three pledgers" kind should be stated publicly from this model.
- **Post-hoc observation, not a criterion. Re-checked with the Student-t intervals; it still holds.**
  - Above f*, committed GAP sustains slightly more normal-agent cooperation than committed BADGE or TFT
    under β = 1, at f ≥ 0.15.
  - Well-mixed: 0.80 to 0.83 for GAP vs 0.74 to 0.78 for BADGE and TFT.
  - Graph: 0.80 to 0.84 vs 0.74 to 0.80. The intervals do not overlap at any of these f.
  - It is a difference in the level of cooperation, not in the threshold.
  - It is fully explained by self-play. The plateau values equal the cooperation of a committed type
    against itself: GAP–GAP 0.801 against BADGE–BADGE 0.738, and 0.758 against 0.730 in the
    even-noise setting. Once most agents hold the committed type, they check each other.
  - So it measures the check in a room that is already willing, not the "turn the room" question.
  - It was not pre-registered and should be treated as a hypothesis, not a finding.

## 8. Limitations

- **Scope.** Every REFUTED is about this restricted model: TFT-based checkers, a single misreading
  channel, and no post-perception check. The full registered conjecture contains mechanisms this model
  does not, and those remain untested.
- **Understanding is not separable from a lower misreading rate here.** A misreading-only cut at the
  same cost is GAP exactly (§4). Testing "understanding beats paying for fewer errors" needs a model in
  which understanding does something a rate cut cannot.
- **Criterion 3 was not testable** with a room of unwilling defectors (§6).
- **Two error sources, not three.** The spec names implementation, perception and understanding errors.
  Here, perception and understanding are one "misreading" of the partner's last move. A pre-move
  understanding error and a post-perception check (spec stage 2, second check placement) are not
  modelled separately.
- **Checkers are TFT-based by design.** A checker built on a forgiving base ("verify *and* forgive") was
  not tested, because it is outside the conjecture as stated. It is the obvious next model.
- **Consent rule is "partner must be willing".** "One checker suffices" was not run.
- **The badge only signals willingness to be checked.** There is no tag assortment, no trust premium,
  and no Hoffman, Yoeli & Nowak "looking penalty".
- **Apology overrides misreading** (§1). This favours APOLOGY, which never won a cell.
- **PPAV is a memory-2 approximation.** The refutation holds without it (§3).
- **Fixed settings:** δ = 0.9 only (no long-game r = 50 case); N = 100 for the embedded chain; γ = 1 only.
- **Experiment 5 scope:** two noise settings, one cost point, one graph (RRG k = 4, N = 1000, 5 seeds);
  b = 1.1 not run.
- **Uncertainty source.** Exact payoffs and exact fixation probabilities mean the only uncertainty in
  experiments 2 to 4 is sensitivity to the grid. Experiment 5 uncertainty is run-to-run spread over 5 to
  10 seeds.
- **Unit choice: run as a post-hoc sensitivity (`posthoc_costFromRound2`).** Costs are charged every
  move, round 1 included. Charging only from round 2 cuts the per-round cost to δ·κ, a 10% cut, for
  every checker.
  - Criterion 2 is unchanged: 0 robust wins, and 0 floor cells where GAP is top under any β and q.
  - That holds with the floor on κ as registered (765 floor cells), and with the floor on the effective
    cost δ·κ (678 floor cells).
  - GAP becomes strictly the most abundant in 14 rows instead of 11, all of them below the floor.
  - Winners at β = 1, q = 0.9: PPAV 1,080, FAKER 388, CTFT 108, ALLD 44.

**Closed forms:**
- TFT vs TFT = b − c.
- ALLD vs ALLC = (b, −c).
- TFT vs ALLD = −c(1−δ); GTFT vs ALLD = −c[(1−δ) + δg].
- WSLS vs ALLD = −c/(1+δ); PPAV vs ALLD = −c(1−δ)/(1−δ³).
- FAKE_APOL vs APOLOGY = (b−γ, −c+γ).
- APOLOGY vs APOLOGY under slips only = (b−c)(1−ε_s); under misreadings only = TFT vs TFT.
- ALLC vs ALLC under slips = (b−c)(1−ε_s).
- TFT vs ALLC under misreadings and under slips.

**Check mechanics:**
- GAP vs GAP equals TFT with ε_m(1−sq), minus κ. PARA equals TFT with ε_m(1−q), minus r.
- No check against unwilling partners. Checks never remove slips.
- BADGE is checked but never checks.
- CTRL's cost matches GAP's, and so does its error cut **per round**. A one-round encounter gives equal
  cooperation.
- `checkableFrom` equals `buildMatrices(ALL, p, 1)` for every pair.
- The apology-overrides-misreading assumption is asserted.
- **Cost and consent, hand-computed** (no noise, κ = 0.2), independent of `effective()`:
  - both checkers pay 0.2 per round;
  - an unwilling partner is never checked and nobody pays;
  - only the checker pays: BADGE and FAKER are checked for free;
  - m = 0.5 halves the cost;
  - charging from round 2 gives a cost of δ·κ.
- CTRL_M equals GAP against every partner.
