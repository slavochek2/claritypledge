# Coaching Track (C-track)

## What Belongs Here

**Definition:** Work that validates workshop revenue, skill building, and team alignment through coaching delivery.

**Classification criteria:**
- Features that enable or improve workshop delivery
- Tools for coaches to teach calibrated communication
- Infrastructure for in-person or remote coaching sessions
- Revenue validation through paid workshops

**Examples:**
- C1: Stories + Live + Events (workshop foundation)
- C2: First Workshops (donation-based pilot)
- C3: Paid Workshops (€5K/month revenue target)

---

## Time Horizon

**0-6 months** — Safety track

The coaching track is the **SAFETY net**. If recognition fails, workshops can generate revenue quickly.

**Characteristics:**
- Short time to revenue (can start workshops immediately)
- Proven demand (existing coaching market)
- Lower scale ceiling (time-for-money model)

---

## Resource Constraints

**Required capabilities:**
- Facilitation skills (already have)
- Event infrastructure (/live, pairing, rooms)
- Workshop curriculum (calibration exercises)
- Payment processing (for paid tier)

**Not required:**
- Large audience (workshops work with 6-12 people)
- Technical depth (teaching > writing)
- Network effects (workshops are 1:N, not N:N)

---

## Decision Framework

**Choose C-track if the work:**
- Enables or improves workshop delivery
- Validates workshop business model
- Solves coach-specific problems (pairing, exercises, feedback)
- Tests pricing or revenue hypotheses

**Don't choose C-track if:**
- Work is about content/essays → R-track
- Work requires scale to validate → X-track
- Work is far-future capability → V-track
- Work is pure infrastructure → foundation

---

## Success Criteria

**Track succeeds if:**
- €5K/month workshop revenue achieved (C3 outcome)
- 3+ coaches adopt protocol (O-Workshop-Retention)
- Workshops validate product UX (qualitative feedback)

**Track fails if:**
- No coaches book paid workshops after 6 months
- Workshop feedback reveals fundamental UX problems
- Revenue stays below €2K/month (not sustainable)
