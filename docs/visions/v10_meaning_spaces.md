# v10 — Meaning spaces

*Captured 2026-09-23. Long-range vision, not a hypothesis: nothing here is testable yet.*

## The analogy

Before writing, language drifted so fast that grandparents and grandchildren could barely understand each other. Written language and dictionaries slowed the drift of **words**. The next step is to stabilise **meaning**: not just which word we use, but whether we verifiably mean the same thing by it.

## The idea

A **meaning space** is a set of words and phrases where two people both know they share the same meaning, and know that the other knows. Today that takes one-on-one work: "what do you mean by cognitive understanding?", paraphrase back, both rate 10.

In the future it happens at scale. People verify meanings in classrooms, teams and events, and that verification is recorded. Two strangers can then see from each other's profiles that they already share a meaning space, and talk without re-establishing it.

It has to start with a word that matters. **Understanding** comes first, and specifically telling cognitive understanding apart from the other kinds. If we do not share the meaning of "understand", asking "how much do you understand me, 0–10?" returns a different answer (often "I don't agree") than the one asked.
