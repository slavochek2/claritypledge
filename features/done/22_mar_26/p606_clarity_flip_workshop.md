# P606: The Clarity Flip — Workshop Anti-Point Design & Facilitation Prep

**Related:** P567 (false belief curriculum), H-WorkshopFormat, H-WTP-Pain
**No code changes required** — this is facilitation design using existing content.
**Format reference:** [facilitator-guide.md](../../../docs/facilitator-guide.md) > Workshop Format: The Clarity Flip

## Problem

The Clarity Flip workshop format is designed but the anti-points that drive the norm inversion haven't been selected or tweaked. The format requires specific anti-points about *verification itself* (not about understanding generally like P567) — beliefs that hold the "asking for paraphrase is awkward" norm in place.

P567's 9 false beliefs are about understanding. The Clarity Flip needs false beliefs specifically about *verification and checking* — the social norm layer, not the cognitive layer.

## Solution

### 1. Select/Design Anti-Points for the Flip

The Clarity Flip needs 4-5 anti-points specifically about verification norms. Candidates (from conversation 2026-03-29):

| # | Anti-point (belief holding old norm) | Why it dies | P567 overlap? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Asking someone to paraphrase implies you think they're stupid" | Stories of partnerships that failed from unchecked assumptions | P567 #6 partial |
| 2 | "Trust means not needing to check" | Trust is BUILT by checking, not assumed | P567 #7 partial |
| 3 | "Verification kills conversational flow" | Protocol takes 2 minutes; misalignment costs months | New |
| 4 | "Strong partnerships are built on intuitive understanding" | Intuition is miscalibrated (r=.178) | New |
| 5 | "If we agree, checking is unnecessary" | Agreement without verification is false agreement | P567 #2 partial |

[FOUNDER DECISION: Which anti-points to include? The above are candidates. Final selection should be based on which ones produce the strongest norm flip — from "awkward to ask" to "awkward to refuse."]

### 2. Design Card Mechanics

- Physical cards (not digital) — commitment before revelation
- Side A: "Is it awkward to REQUEST paraphrase?" (1-10)
- Side B: "Is it awkward to REFUSE paraphrase request without reason?" (1-10)
- Prediction mechanic: predict room average before reveal (surfaces pluralistic ignorance)

### 3. Workshop Run Sheet (90 min)

| Time | Phase | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | The Question | Card Side A scores, face down |
| 5-15 | Reveal + Predict | Show scores, compare to predictions. Gap = pluralistic ignorance |
| 15-35 | Surface Anti-Points | Collect reasons verification feels awkward. Write on whiteboard |
| 35-55 | Stories Kill Anti-Points | 3-4 participant stories. Cross out anti-points that die |
| 55-65 | Common Knowledge Move | "Raise hand if verification is valuable." Pause. "Did you know they all thought this?" |
| 65-75 | Pairs Practice | 2 min each: share belief → paraphrase → correct → switch |
| 75-85 | The Flip | Card Side B scores. Reveal. Norm has inverted. |
| 85-90 | Homework | Write one Clarity Letter to someone not in the room |

### 4. Facilitation Notes

- Never lecture. Ask, they discover. Never say "false beliefs are bad."
- Stories section makes or breaks it. Push for concrete: "What specifically did you think they understood?"
- The common knowledge moment needs silence. Don't explain Pinker. Let them feel it.
- Photo the anti-points list with crossed-out items = instrument development data.
- After 10 sessions: pattern data on which beliefs die fastest vs stubborn ones.

### 5. Post-Workshop

- H-WTP-Pain test: use refined questions (1) "Can you share a past experience where a misunderstanding cost you something real?" (2) "How would you describe to a friend how our clarity session helped you?"
- Offer de-risking for pairs
- Invite to Clarity Practice Community (P603) if Partner Agreement signed
