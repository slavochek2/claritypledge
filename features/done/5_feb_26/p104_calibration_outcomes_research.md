# P: Calibration Outcomes Research

**Status:** ✅ Complete
**Created:** 2026-01-28
**Goal:** Validate foundational assumption that calibration connects to business outcomes

---

## Executive Summary

**The ground is mostly solid, but with fault lines.**

| Finding | Implication |
|---------|-------------|
| Strong meta-analytic evidence (r=.39, N=400K) linking perceived listening to work outcomes | The relationship exists |
| Strongest links: sales performance, wellbeing | Focus pitch here |
| Weakest link: innovation | Don't oversell this |
| 75% of research relies on self-reports | Measurement gap = our opportunity |
| Teach-back works in healthcare (60% fewer readmissions) | Best proof point for verification loops |
| Training transfer problem (12% apply skills) | Workshops fail; embedded practice wins |
| No existing tool measures if listener actually understood | Clear market gap |

**Strategic verdict:** Proceed with confidence, but don't oversell causal claims. Our differentiation is measurement itself.

## Track A: Value of Calibration ✅

### Summary Table

| Outcome | Evidence | Effect Size | Causal? | Counter-Evidence |
|---------|----------|-------------|---------|------------------|
| **Sales performance** | Strong | r=.38-.50 | Mostly correlational | Some null studies; direction unclear |
| **Employee retention** | Mixed | Not quantified | Some quasi-experimental | Survey data dominates |
| **Team performance** | Mixed | 43% variance (Google) | Correlational | Not peer-reviewed |
| **Employee wellbeing** | Strong | r=.28 (burnout) | Some experimental | Third variable concerns |
| **Innovation** | Weak | Not quantified | Correlational | Research is about involvement, not listening |

### Key Research Findings

**Meta-analysis (Kluger et al., 2023):**
- 664 effect sizes, 400,020 observations
- Perceived listening → work outcomes: r=.39
- Relationship quality: r=.51
- Performance: r=.36
- Limitation: 75% self-report data

**Sales:**
- Salesperson listening → sales volume: r=.38 (corrected: ρ=.47)
- Customer-perceived listening → composite performance: r=.50

**Wellbeing:**
- Perceived listening → reduced burnout: r=.28 (meta-analytic)
- Listening training → reduced turnover intentions (quasi-experimental)

**Healthcare (Teach-back):**
- Heart failure readmissions reduced 60% (OR=0.40) with teach-back
- Strong experimental evidence in healthcare; untested in workplace

### Counter-Evidence Found

1. **Training transfer problem:** Only 12% of employees apply new skills learned in training
2. **Generic training fails:** "Companies spend £50,000 on communication workshop... six weeks later, nothing changed"
3. **Research quality:** Most studies correlational, self-report
4. **Null results exist:** Some studies found no significant relationship between supervisor listening and performance

### How Coaches Assess Today

1. **Self-assessment questionnaires** (most common, but self-reported)
2. **360 feedback** (listening is 1 of 30 items, annual, generic)
3. **Observation during sessions** (subjective, not scalable)
4. **Gut feeling** (no data)
5. **Nothing** (most common honest answer)

| **No mutual verification** | All tools one-directional |

## Synthesis

### Overall Evidence Assessment

**✅ Mixed-to-Strong — Proceed with confidence, but calibrate claims**

The foundational assumption (calibration matters for outcomes) is supported by substantial correlational evidence and some experimental evidence in healthcare. The weakest link is innovation; the strongest are sales and wellbeing.

### Key Insight

> **The measurement is the moat.** Everyone believes listening matters, but no one measures actual understanding. Academic instruments exist but aren't productized. Sales tools measure talk time. Coaches use gut feeling. We're filling a real gap.

### Implications for Product

1. **Don't sell as training** — Training transfer fails. Sell as practice system embedded in real meetings.
2. **Healthcare teach-back is the proof point** — Use this analogy: "Like teach-back in hospitals reduced readmissions 60%, verification in meetings reduces miscommunication"
3. **Don't oversell causal claims** — Say "associated with" not "causes"
4. **Psychological safety is prerequisite** — Tool creates safety for verification; don't assume it

### Implications for Coach Pitch

1. **"You see their blindspot, they don't"** — Validated insight
2. **"No tool measures actual understanding"** — Market gap confirmed
3. **"Self-assessment doesn't work"** — Research supports this
4. **"Talk-time ≠ understanding"** — Differentiates from Gong/Chorus
5. **Use healthcare parallel** — Teach-back is proven, we're applying same principle

### Risks Acknowledged

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Workplace evidence is mostly correlational | Don't oversell; collect our own outcome data |
| Training transfer problem | Product design: embedded practice, not workshops |
| Self-report bias in research | Our tool provides behavioral data |
| No direct evidence for verification loops in workplace | Healthcare parallel; be first to test |
