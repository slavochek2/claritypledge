# P106: Demo Kit

**Goal:** 3-5 prepared ideas for /live demos that work without prior context

---

## Why This Exists

/live works when one party has prepared content to share. Ad-hoc doesn't work well.

For coach validation and demos, we need ready-to-use ideas that:
- Are clear enough to explain in 2-3 sentences
- Have predictable misunderstandings
- Demonstrate the calibration gap

---

## Demo Ideas (From Article)

### 1. The Clarity Tax

**The idea (share this):**
> "The Clarity Tax is the hidden cost organizations pay when people assume they understood each other without checking. It's a coordination failure externality — the person who refuses to verify understanding keeps their flexibility, but the team pays the cost in rework and confusion."

**Common misunderstandings:**
- "It's just about being clear when you speak" (No — it's about VERIFYING understanding)
- "It's about miscommunication" (No — it's about the COST of unverified assumptions)

**Good playback sounds like:**
- Mentions: cost/tax, verification, team pays while individual benefits

---

### 2. Verified Alignment vs Illusion of Shared Reality

**The idea (share this):**
> "Most conversations end with everyone feeling like they understood each other. This is the Illusion of Shared Reality. Verified Alignment is different — it requires explicit work: playing back what you heard, asking 'do you mean X or Y?', and getting confirmation. The illusion feels good but costs you later."

**Common misunderstandings:**
- "If I feel like I understood, I probably did" (No — that's the illusion)
- "Asking for clarification means I wasn't listening" (No — it's the opposite)

**Good playback sounds like:**
- Distinguishes feeling understood from VERIFIED understanding
- Mentions explicit work required

---

### 3. The Understanding Gap

**The idea (share this):**
> "The Understanding Gap is the difference between how confident you are that you understood someone, and how accurate your understanding actually was. Most people are overconfident — they rate themselves 8/10 when the speaker would rate them 5/10. Seeing this number is often a surprise."

**Common misunderstandings:**
- "This measures how well someone explains" (No — it measures understanding calibration)
- "Lower gap is always better" (Nuance: gap direction matters too)

**Good playback sounds like:**
- Mentions the comparison: confidence vs accuracy
- Mentions overconfidence as the typical pattern

---

### 4. Mental Models

**The idea (share this):**
> "Everyone carries mental models — internal maps of what words and concepts mean. When you say 'agile development,' your map might include daily standups and sprints. Your colleague's map might mean flexibility and less documentation. Same words, different worlds. Without verification, you discover the mismatch too late."

**Common misunderstandings:**
- "This is about vocabulary/definitions" (Partly — but it's about entire frameworks)
- "Smart people have aligned mental models" (No — expertise often increases divergence)

**Good playback sounds like:**
- Mentions internal maps / different interpretations
- Mentions discovery of mismatch coming too late

---

### 5. The Golden Rule of Communication (From 6.1 Paper)

**The idea (share this):**
> "The Clarity Pledge is the Golden Rule applied to understanding: 'Verify others as you would want to be verified.' If you want people to check whether they understood you correctly, you should do the same for them. It's not about doubting intelligence — it's about respecting that understanding is hard."

**Common misunderstandings:**
- "Asking for playback implies you think they're stupid" (No — it implies you care about accuracy)
- "This slows everything down" (Short-term yes, long-term saves rework)

**Good playback sounds like:**
- Connects to Golden Rule / reciprocity
- Frames verification as respect, not doubt

---

## How to Use in Demo

1. **Pick one idea** (start with #3 Understanding Gap — most concrete)
2. **Share it** (read or paraphrase the idea above)
3. **Ask for playback** ("Can you explain back what you understood?")
4. **Rate together** (Listener: confidence 1-10, Speaker: accuracy 1-10)
5. **Discuss the gap** (This IS the product demo)

---

## What We Learn

After each demo, note:
- Which idea worked best?
- What misunderstandings occurred?
- Was the gap surprising to them?
- Did they see value in the process?
