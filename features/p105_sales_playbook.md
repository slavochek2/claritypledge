## The Model: Be Your Own Coach First

### The Evolution

**v1 (2026-01-28):** Coaches pay $50-100/month → Rejected (small market, doesn't prove value)

**v2 (2026-01-29):** Coaches as partners → Superseded (still depends on convincing coaches)

**v3 (2026-02-03):** Be your own coach first → **Current**

### Current Model

```
You (as coach/trainer) → Run events (donation-based) → Participants get value →
  → Prove tool works → Subscription revenue from participants/teams/businesses
```

**Why this model:**
- No dependency on convincing coaches
- Learn fastest by doing it yourself
- Build case studies with your own events
- Revenue comes later, after value is proven
- Psychologically freed from "must sell to others" pressure

### What You're Testing

| Hypothesis | Success Signal |
|------------|----------------|
| Events create value | Participants say so, come back |
| Tool adds value beyond you | Someone else runs event with tool, it works |
| Path to subscription exists | WTP signal at some price point |

### Events Strategy

**Format:** Online workshops, donation-based, 60-90 min

**Content:**
- Theory: Active listening, explain-back protocol, why calibration matters
- Practice: Participants verify understanding with each other using /live
- Reflection: What did you notice? What surprised you?

**Goal per event:**
- Participants experience their calibration gap
- Collect feedback on value
- Build content for newsletter
- Identify who might pay for more

## The Full System

The product isn't just /live measurement — it's a complete system:

```
1. PLEDGE / COMMITMENT
   └── Creates social permission to use explain-back
   └── Removes awkwardness ("We've both committed")

2. TEACH-BACK / EXPLAIN-BACK
   └── The interaction mechanism
   └── Listener plays back understanding

3. CALIBRATION MEASUREMENT
   └── Confidence vs verification = gap revealed
   └── Track improvement over time

4. TRAINING / THEORY
   └── You bring legitimacy and epistemology
   └── Explains WHY this works

5. CO-ORGANIZED EVENTS
   └── Coach brings clients/credibility
   └── You bring facilitation/tool
   └── Both learn together
```

## The Insight

> **The tool reveals a blindspot people don't know they have.**
>
> The person who's blind won't pay — but the person who SEES the blindspot (the coach) will partner.

Coaches see their clients' blindspots:
- **Understanding miscalibration:** Client thinks "I understood" but didn't
- **Proof problem:** Coach sees the gap but can't measure it objectively
- **Improvement problem:** Can't track improvement over time
- **Spread problem:** Client can't prove it to their team

**Terminology:** With coaches, use "listening calibration" (their word). Internally: "understanding calibration" (precise). See [definitions.md](../docs/definitions.md).

### Path from Coaches to Vision

```
Coaches (partners) → Clients (pay) → Teams (pay more) → Organizations → Democratic deliberation
```

Charge where value lands. Coaches are distribution + learning + collaboration.

## Value Articulation

### Pain-Based Pitch (Problem → Cost)

> "Your clients' teams lose approximately $12,500 per employee annually to miscommunication — that's 21% of salary. For a 10-person team, $125,000/year.
>
> Here's the blindspot: listeners' minds wander 24% of the time, but speakers think they're being heard. Research shows people's ability to know what they understood is nearly random.
>
> Current tools (Gong, 360) measure how much you talk. Nobody measures whether understanding actually happened.
>
> I have a tool that does. Want to see your own calibration gap?"

### Value-Based Pitch (Solution → Outcome)

> "The teach-back method — having listeners explain back what they understood — reduces errors by 45-60% in healthcare. Same mechanism.
>
> Listening correlates with sales performance, reduced burnout, and team effectiveness. Google found psychological safety accounts for 43% of team performance variance.
>
> I measure the gap between 'I think I understood' and 'I actually understood.' When people see their gap, they improve. When improvement is tracked, it sticks.
>
> Let's run an event together. Your clients practice, we both learn, and we build a case study."

### ROI Framework (For Business Sales)

```
Cost of miscommunication:     $12,506/employee/year (Grammarly 2024)
10-person team:               $125,060/year
Even 10% improvement:         $12,506 saved
Tool cost:                    $6,000/year ($500/month)
Minimum ROI:                  2x
```

## Hypotheses to Validate

### Core Hypothesis (Updated 2026-01-29)

> Coaches will PARTNER to co-run events using the calibration tool. Value delivered to participants (clients, teams). Revenue comes from where value lands — participants or their companies. Coaches are partners, not customers.

**Pricing model:** Coaches = partners (free). Participants/businesses = customers (pay).

### Previous Hypothesis (Superseded)

> ~~Coaches will USE a tool that measures their clients' understanding calibration gap. If clients spread it to teams, BUSINESSES pay. If not, coaches may pay.~~

**Why superseded:** €100/month from a coach proves nothing. Partnership + successful events = real validation.

### Hypothesis Hierarchy

**Tier 1: Pain (Must Validate)**

| # | Hypothesis | Status | How to Validate |
|---|------------|--------|-----------------|
| H-Biz-1 | Coaches have clients with understanding miscalibration | ⬜ | "Do you have clients who think they understand but don't?" |
| H-Biz-2 | This is a priority problem | ⬜ | They describe specific painful situations |
| H-Biz-3 | No tool measures it (measurement was impossible) | ⬜ | "Have you ever been able to MEASURE that gap?" |

*If Tier 1 fails: Stop. Problem doesn't exist or isn't painful enough.*

**Tier 2: Usage (Validates Distribution)**

| # | Hypothesis | Status | How to Validate |
|---|------------|--------|-----------------|
| H-Biz-4 | Clients would trust coach-provided data | ⬜ | "Would your client believe the data?" |
| H-Biz-5 | Coaches would use ongoing (retention) | ⬜ | "Every client, or just once?" |
| H-Biz-6 | Coach open to own measurement | ⬜ | "Would you want to see YOUR calibration too?" |

*If Tier 2 passes: Coaches will use it. Now validate who pays.*

**Tier 3: Spread & Revenue (Determines Business Model)**

| # | Hypothesis | Status | How to Validate |
|---|------------|--------|-----------------|
| H-Biz-7 | **Spread signal** — clients want for teams | ⬜ | "Would your client want this for their team?" |
| H-Biz-8 | **Business WTP** — company would pay $500/team/mo | ⬜ | "Would their company pay $500/month for the team?" |
| H-Biz-9 | **Coach WTP** — coaches would pay $50-100/mo | ⬜ | "Would YOU pay $75/month for this?" |
| H-Biz-10 | Provides differentiation | ⬜ | "Would this help you win clients?" |

**Decision logic:**
- If H-Biz-7 (spread) = YES → Coaches are distributors, businesses pay (H-Biz-8)
- If H-Biz-7 (spread) = NO → Coaches are customers (H-Biz-9)

*Tier 3 determines the entire business model.*

### Concerns to Validate

**Trust Problem (clarified):**
The trust problem is NOT "does explain-back work?" (research validates it does).

The trust problem IS: **Will clients trust calibration feedback that comes from their coach?**
- Coach has a stake in the outcome
- Client might think "coach is just selling me their coaching"
- Calibration from a single source (coach) may not be believed

**Solution:** Third-party verification solves this:
- Events with multiple coaches (peers verify each other)
- Client accumulates calibration from many partners over time
- The metric becomes credible through volume, not single source

**Retention Problem:**
- If coaches say "I'd use it ongoing with same client" → Retention exists
- If coaches say "just once per client" → But protocol-led growth creates ongoing value:
  - Client teaches team → ongoing team use
  - Calibration history becomes trust signal → ongoing value
  - Platform membership = psychological safety → ongoing membership

## Event Guide

### Event Format

**Co-organized event (Zoom/Meet), 60-90 minutes**
- You: facilitation, theory, tool
- Coach: brings clients, adds context, participates

### Event Invite Message

**From you to coach (recruiting founding member):**
```
Hi [Name],

I'm looking for coaches to partner with on something experimental.

I've built a tool that measures "listening calibration" — the gap between
how well people think they understood vs. how well they actually did.
Research shows this gap costs $12,500/employee/year in miscommunication.

Instead of pitching you a subscription, I want to co-run an event with
your clients. You bring the context and relationships, I bring the tool
and facilitation. We both learn, we both get a case study.

Interested in exploring?

— Slava
```

**From coach to clients (once partnered):**
```
Hi [Name],

I'm co-running a session on "calibrated listening" with a researcher
who's built a tool to measure it. We'll practice a protocol where you
experience your own calibration gap — the difference between how well
you think you understand others vs. how well you actually do.

[Date/Time] | 60-90 min | Limited spots

Interested?

— [Coach]
```

| Step | Time | What Happens |
|------|------|--------------|
| 1. Welcome + context | 10 min | Why calibrated listening matters for coaching |
| 2. Teach protocol | 10 min | Explain-back protocol theory |
| 3. Demo /live | 15 min | You share idea, one coach plays back, show the gap |
| 4. Coaches pair up | 20 min | They verify each other, experience their own gap |
| 5. Debrief | 15 min | What did you notice? How does this compare? |
| 6. Ask | 10 min | "Would you pay $50-100/month for this with clients?" |

**Why event > 1-on-1:**
- Coaches experience their OWN miscalibration (not just hear about it)
- Third-party verification (peers, not you) solves trust problem
- Multiple data points in one session
- Registration = users on platform
- You're facilitating, not selling

### Alternative: 1-on-1 Discovery (if events don't work)

| Step | What You Do |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Ask about pain** | "Do you have clients who think they understand others well but actually don't?" |
| **1b. Ask about measurement** | "Have you ever been able to MEASURE that gap — not their listening behavior, but whether they actually understood? Or can you only assert it?" |
| **2. Listen** | Let them talk. Take notes. Don't pitch. |
| **3. Explore current solutions** | "How do you currently show them the gap?" |
| **4. If pain exists, demo** | "Can I show you something I'm building? 2 minutes." |
| **5. Ask about trust** | "If you showed them this data, would they believe it?" |
| **6. Ask about retention** | "Would you use this once per client or ongoing?" |
| **7. Ask about spread** | "If this helped your client, would they want it for their team?" |
| **8. Ask business WTP** | "Would their company pay $500/month for the team to use this?" |
| **9. Ask coach WTP** | "Would YOU pay $75/month for this with your clients?" |
| **10. Pricing model signal** | If spread (7) = yes → business pays. If spread = no → coach pays. |
| **11. If positive, propose pilot** | "Would you try it with one client? I'd love to see if it helps." |

### What NOT to Say

- Don't promise features that don't exist
- Don't pitch — explore
- Don't defend — learn
- Don't mention: Stories, Points, Sifter, events, community (all deferred)
