## 1. Viral Loop: Teach-Back Cascade

### The Mechanism

```
Coach teaches explain-back to client
    ↓
Client experiences calibration revelation ("I thought I understood but didn't")
    ↓
Client uses explain-back with their team
    ↓
Team members experience same revelation
    ↓
Some become advocates, spread further
```

### Is This Our Growth Engine?

**Yes, potentially.** The explain-back protocol is:
- Simple to learn (can be taught in 5 minutes)
- Immediately valuable (works in any conversation)
- Self-demonstrating (the gap reveals itself)
- Shareable (doesn't require the tool to practice)

### The Risk

**Protocol spreads, but tool doesn't.** People can do explain-back without Clarity Pledge. What locks them into the product?

**Answer:** Measurement + tracking over time. The protocol is free. The calibration data is the product.

### Implication for Coaches

Coaches become **distributors of the protocol**, not just users of the tool. Their clients spread it. This is aligned with their mission (help people communicate better) and our growth.

**Possible positioning:** "Teach your clients explain-back. We'll measure if it's working."

## 4. Psychological Safety & Onboarding

### The Problem

Research shows:
- Training fails without psychological safety
- People won't admit they didn't understand if they fear judgment
- The tool requires vulnerability ("I might have misunderstood you")

### Do We Need Onboarding?

**Yes.** But not feature onboarding — **mindset onboarding.**

### What Onboarding Should Do

1. **Normalize the gap:** "Most people overestimate their understanding. You're not broken."
2. **Frame verification as strength:** "Checking understanding is smart, not weak."
3. **Create safety:** "The speaker can't see your confidence rating. This is for you."
4. **Start with low stakes:** First /live should be with someone safe (friend, coach, trusted colleague)

### When Does Onboarding Happen?

| Moment | What Happens |
|--------|--------------|
| **First visit** | Landing page frames it correctly (not "you're bad at listening" but "everyone has this gap") |
| **Before first /live** | Brief setup: "This works best when both people feel safe to be honest" |
| **First gap reveal** | Celebrate, don't shame: "You discovered something! This is how calibration improves." |
| **Coach-led sessions** | Coach creates safety. We don't need to. |

### Implication for Coach Model

**Coaches are the safety creators.** When coaches bring clients into /live, they handle the psychological safety. We don't need heavy onboarding for coach-led use.

**Self-serve users need more onboarding.** If someone tries /live without a coach, we need to create safety ourselves.

## 5. Strongest References

### For Sales Claims

| Claim | Source | Why Strong |
|-------|--------|------------|
| r=.47 listening → sales volume | Kluger et al. 2023 | Meta-analysis, 16 studies, N=3,780 |
| r=.50 customer-perceived listening → performance | Bergeron & Laroche 2009 | Used company sales records, not self-report |

**Best quote:** "Salesperson listening and sales volume correlated at r=.38 (corrected ρ=.47)" — Kluger et al. 2023

### For Wellbeing Claims

| Claim | Source | Why Strong |
|-------|--------|------------|
| r=.28 listening → reduced burnout | Kluger et al. 2023 | Meta-analysis, K=10, N=3,858 |
| Listening training → reduced turnover intentions | Itzchakov 2023 | Quasi-experimental (not just correlation) |

**Best quote:** "Perceived listening negatively associated with burnout, r=0.28. This effect size is in the top third of correlations in applied psychology." — Kluger et al. 2023

### For Verification/Teach-Back

| Claim | Source | Why Strong |
|-------|--------|------------|
| 60% reduction in hospital readmissions | Heart failure meta-analysis | OR=0.40, experimental (RCTs) |
| Effective across settings and populations | PLOS One systematic review | Broad validation |

**Best quote:** "Discharge education using teach-back significantly reduced readmission rates (OR=0.40, 95%CI=[0.17, 0.94])" — Heart failure meta-analysis

## 6. Market Gap Positioning

### Against "Nothing" (Most Common Competitor)

> "Your team probably doesn't measure understanding at all. You assume people got it. Sometimes they didn't. Now there's a way to know."

### Against Gong/Chorus

> "Gong tells you how much you talked. We tell you if they understood. Talk-time ≠ comprehension."

### Against 360 Feedback

> "In 360 feedback, listening is one checkbox among thirty, rated once a year by people guessing. We measure actual understanding, in real conversations, verified by the speaker."

### Against Self-Assessment

> "Self-assessment doesn't work. People overestimate their listening. That's the whole problem. We use speaker verification — the only person who knows if you understood is the person you were trying to understand."
