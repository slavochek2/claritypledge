### Key Terminology Finding

## Track A: Measurement Gap Validation ✅

**Verdict: VALIDATED**

### Existing Academic Measures (Lab Only)

| Measure | Field | Limitation for Conversations |
|---------|-------|------------------------------|
| Metacomprehension Accuracy | Educational psych | Designed for text reading, not spoken dialogue |
| Judgment of Learning (JOL) | Learning science | Lab-based, not real-time |
| Listening Fidelity (LF) | Communication | Lab task (drawing reproduction), not transferable |
| Metacognitive Listening Strategies | Communication | Self-report only, no verification |

### Commercial Tools Evaluated (None Measure Calibration)

| Tool | What It Actually Measures | Why NOT Calibration |
|------|---------------------------|---------------------|
| Read AI | Sentiment, engagement, speaker talking speed | Measures speaker behavior — no listener verification |
| Poised | Speakshare, clarity, filler words | Speaker-only coaching |
| Gong | Deal risk, keywords, talk ratios | Sales analytics — not understanding |
| Glisn | Active listening training | Solo practice — no live speaker verification |

### The Missing Link

```
Current tools measure:
Speaker → [Speaks] → Metrics (filler words, pace)
                   ↓
               Listener → [Listens] → ??? (no verification)

What Clarity Pledge adds:
Speaker → [Speaks] → Story
                   ↓
               Listener → [Confidence Rating]
                                    ↓
                               Speaker → [Verifies] → Calibration Gap Revealed
```

### Key Research Support

Collins et al. (2023, Harvard/UCLA) proved the problem:
- Speakers correctly identify whether they're being heard only **70% of the time**
- When listeners admitted to mind wandering, **nearly 80% of speakers still felt heard**
- Nonverbal cues (nodding, eye contact) effectively mask inattention

**The research proves the problem but offers no product.** We fill this gap.

## Track B: Calibration → Outcomes ✅

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VALIDATED**

### Outcome-Evidence Table

| Outcome | Evidence Level | Effect Size | Causal? |
|---------|----------------|-------------|---------|
| **Learning/Test Performance** | Strong | r=.33, g=0.565 | Yes (RCTs exist) |
| **Study Behavior/Regulation** | Strong | γ=.70 | Yes (experimental) |
| **Decision-Making Quality** | Moderate | Qualitative | Correlational |
| **Work Performance** | Moderate | 5-30% manufacturing costs | Correlational |
| **Error/Rework Reduction** | Moderate | $280K examples | Correlational |
| **Relationships/Trust** | Weak | No direct effect sizes | Theoretical |
| **Collaboration/Teams** | Weak | Counterevidence exists | Mixed |

### Strongest Links

1. **Learning Outcomes:** Thiede et al. (2003) showed accurate calibration → strategic restudy → better test performance. AI-powered calibration RCT (2025): 8.9% learning gains (p=.019).

2. **Decision-Making:** "No problem in judgment and decision making is more prevalent and more potentially catastrophic than overconfidence" (Plous). The "illusion of information adequacy" leads to decisions with incomplete information.

### Effect Size Comparison

| Measure | Effect Size |
|---------|-------------|
| Listening → Outcomes (P104) | r=.39 |
| Self-estimated vs measured intelligence | r=.33 |
| Baseline metacomprehension accuracy | r=.178 |
| Calibration interventions | g=0.46-0.72 |

**Key insight:** Baseline calibration (r=.178) is weaker than listening (r=.39), BUT calibration can be dramatically improved through interventions.

### Gap for P110

No existing research measures: **understanding calibration in conversation → relational/work outcomes**. This is our novel contribution.

## Track C: Construct Relationships ✅

**Verdict: CONSTRUCTS ARE PARTIALLY LINKED**

### The Multi-Path Model

```
LISTENING (behavior)
     │
     ├────────────────────────┬─────────────────┐
     ▼                        ▼                 │
UNDERSTANDING           RELATIONSHIP            │
(comprehension)         QUALITY                 │
     │                        │                 │
     ▼                        │                 │
CALIBRATION                   │                 │
(metacognitive accuracy)      │                 │
     │                        │                 │
     └────────────────────────┴─────────────────┘
                     │
                     ▼
                 OUTCOMES
```

### Calibration's Role

- **Mediator:** Listening → Calibration → Outcomes (supported by mediation studies)
- **Moderator:** Good calibration amplifies listening's effect (empathic accuracy research)
- **Independent path:** Calibration has direct effects on decision-making

### P104 Evidence Transfer

| P104 Finding | Transfers? | Reasoning |
|--------------|------------|-----------|
| Listening → Relationship quality (r=.51) | YES | Grounding requires calibration |
| Listening → Wellbeing | PARTIALLY | Feeling understood ≠ being understood |
| Training transfer (12%) | NO | Different mechanisms |

### Key Bridging Papers

1. **Deppermann & Mondada (2017):** "Calibrating with micro-sequences in face-to-face dialogue" — 97% of dialogues follow three-step calibration pattern
2. **Clark & Brennan (1991):** Grounding theory — foundational, >2000 citations
3. **Ickes (1993):** Empathic accuracy paradigm — measures accuracy of understanding others

### The Pitch Implication

"Research shows listening matters (P104, r=.39). **But here's the problem:** people systematically misjudge their understanding. The gap between feeling heard and actually being understood is where miscommunication happens. We measure that gap."

## Track D: Interventions ✅

**Verdict: CALIBRATION IS TRAINABLE**

### Intervention-Effectiveness Table

| Intervention | Effect Size | Evidence Quality | Transfer |
|--------------|-------------|------------------|----------|
| Delayed JOL | γ=.93 vs .40 immediate | Strong | Limited |
| Concept Mapping | g=0.72 | Strong (N=11,814) | Moderate |
| Situation-Model | g=0.46 (N=2,236) | Strong (meta-analysis) | Moderate |
| **Teach-Back** | **OR=0.40-0.55 (45% readmission reduction)** | Strong in healthcare | Unknown in workplace |
| Forecasting Training | 6-11% accuracy improvement | Strong (RCT) | Tested geopolitical only |
| All Interventions Combined | g=0.25 | Moderate (35 studies) | Varies |

### Teach-Back Evidence (Our Mechanism)

Healthcare evidence is robust:
- **45% reduction in 30-day readmissions** (OR=0.55)
- **60% reduction in heart failure readmissions** (OR=0.40)
- **95% of studies** reported positive outcomes

**Mechanism:** Forces articulation, reveals gaps immediately, leverages generation effect, creates feedback loop.

**Gap:** No direct workplace studies — healthcare is our proof point.

### Forecasting Parallels (Tetlock)

- One-hour training: **6-11% accuracy improvement**
- Effects **persisted for at least one year**
- Superforecasters: treat beliefs as hypotheses, master perspective-taking

### Trainability Verdict

**YES — calibration is a skill, not a fixed trait:**
- "Metacognition is not hardwired... students can be trained to improve their monitoring"
- Multi-domain training enables transfer
- Feedback is necessary but not sufficient

### What We're Testing (Novel Combination)

1. Explain-back (generation effect)
2. Immediate feedback (gap visibility)
3. Social verification (speaker confirms)
4. Longitudinal tracking (calibration over time)

This combination is untested in workplace communication.

## Strategic Verdict

### Proceed With Confidence

**What to Claim (Backed by Evidence):**
- Communication problems cost real money ($12,500/employee)
- People systematically overestimate their understanding (r=.178)
- The gap between feeling heard and actually being understood exists (UCLA/Harvard)
- Calibration is trainable (g=0.46-0.72)
- Teach-back/verification loops work (45% fewer readmissions)

**What NOT to Claim (Insufficient Evidence):**
- Specific effect sizes for calibration → workplace outcomes
- That our tool specifically improves outcomes (we measure; need outcome data)
- That calibration alone explains listening's effects

### Implications for Coach Validation

1. **Lead with the blindspot:** "You see their calibration gap, they don't"
2. **Use $ figures:** $12,500/employee is concrete and credible
3. **Healthcare parallel:** Teach-back is proven; we're applying same principle
4. **Don't oversell causation:** "Associated with" not "causes"
5. **Position as measurement:** We don't train; we measure

### Next Steps

1. Consider outreach to Collins/Minson/Brooks (UCLA/Harvard Listening Gap researchers) — potential research partnership
2. Collect our own outcome data from coach pilots
3. Track calibration improvement longitudinally
