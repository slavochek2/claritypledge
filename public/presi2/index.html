Why almost nobody verifies understanding

Illusion of transparency

We think our meaning is far more obvious than it is. Tap a song's rhythm: tappers expect 50% to name it; 2.5% do.1

Curse of knowledge

Once you know something, you can't un-know it, which makes it hard to feel what the other person is missing.2

The social norm

Conversation lets people fix their own meaning. Stepping in to check what someone understood is a marked move, so we delay it, soften it, or skip it.3

I actually do the opposite. I always prompt it to be more disagreeable and make it find flaws in my thinking, and when it disagrees it might feel disappointing at first, but I found that in trying to understand its reasoning I either correct my false beliefs or I learn to better express myself to make Claude more aligned. Not through coercion/gaslighting but through carefully deployed reasoning chains. The benefit of this approach is that once I do it I can reuse the conclusions and reasoning later and help it be more aligned with reality as it probably is, not as I wish it to be. This way Claude becomes a partner whose disagreements lead to productive insights. And if there is no insight about the validity and meaning of the claims we discuss and it turns out to be a pure disagreement about values (never happened to me so far), I would gain clarity about my own values.08:52 ✓✓
